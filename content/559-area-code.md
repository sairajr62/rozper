## History of the 559 Area Code

![North American Numbering Plan Pyramid Diagram for California](/images/blog/559-image-3.webp)

### California's 209: The Original Central Valley Code

Before 559 existed, Central California — from Stockton south through Fresno to Bakersfield — was covered by **area code 209**, which had served the San Joaquin Valley since the NANP launched in 1947. As California's agricultural economy industrialized and its cities grew, 209 was split multiple times. By the 1990s, the mobile phone boom and the proliferation of pager and fax lines were driving unprecedented demand for new numbers in the Central Valley.

### November 14, 1998: 559 Created

In 1997, the California-Nevada Code Administration (CNCA) flagged 209 as approaching exhaustion and placed it on a priority relief list alongside several other overloaded California codes. On **November 14, 1998**, area code 559 was created to serve Fresno, Madera, Kings, and Tulare counties. The northern portion of the former 209 zone — Stockton, Modesto, and the northern San Joaquin Valley — retained the 209 prefix.

### March 26, 2025: The 357 Overlay Launches

By the 2020s, explosive growth in mobile devices, VoIP services, business lines, and IoT-connected agricultural technology was pushing the 559 numbering supply toward exhaustion. In 2023, the CPUC approved the **357 area code** as an overlay for the entire 559 territory — a process similar to how the [213 area code](/blog/213-area-code) gained 323 and 738 as overlays to extend Los Angeles's numbering capacity. The new 357 code went into active service on **March 26, 2025**.

The 357 overlay works exactly like similar overlays introduced elsewhere in California:
- Existing 559 numbers remain unchanged — no customer had to change their number.
- New telephone service requests in the region may receive either a 559 or 357 number depending on availability.
- Calling zones, rates, and what constitutes a local call are entirely unaffected.
- **Mandatory 10-digit dialing** became effective in Q1 2025 — every call within the 559/357 zone now requires the full area code + 7-digit number.

## 559 Area Code Time Zone

The **559 area code** is in the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**:

- **Pacific Standard Time (PST):** UTC−8 — observed from early November to mid-March
- **Pacific Daylight Time (PDT):** UTC−7 — observed from mid-March to early November

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**To/from the East Coast (ET):** Eastern Time is 3 hours ahead of Pacific. A Fresno business calling a New York client at 9 AM PT reaches them at noon ET — past their morning; this is a comfortable entry. To reach East Coast contacts before their midday break, call by 9 AM PT.

**To/from Central Time (CT):** CT is 2 hours ahead of PT. Calling Chicago or Dallas from Fresno at 8 AM PT reaches them at 10 AM CT — solid business hours overlap throughout the day.

**To/from Mountain Time (MT):** MT is 1 hour ahead of PT. Best time zone pair for coordination. Note Arizona's no-DST exception during summer months.

**International export calls:** For the San Joaquin Valley's significant export trade to Asia and Mexico, calls to Tokyo (UTC+9) are 17 hours ahead of PT — typically reached during PT morning for Asia-Pacific business day close. Calls to Mexico City operate on a 1–2 hour offset.

Because the 559 zone shares Pacific Time with the major California coastal metros (Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego), intrastate business coordination has no time zone friction.

## Is a 559 Area Code Call a Scam?

The **559 area code** is entirely legitimate and serves real residents and businesses across the Central San Joaquin Valley. However, like all US area codes, it is subject to caller ID spoofing by scammers who exploit the local number preference of Central Valley residents to increase answer rates.

Six scam types are particularly well-documented in the 559 region:

### Fake Amazon Purchase Alerts

This is among the highest-volume scam types targeting 559 residents. Robocalls claim that an expensive purchase — specific amounts like $279.99 for an order or $1,499.00 for an iPhone — has been placed on the recipient's Amazon account and that they must call back or press 1 immediately to cancel it. Amazon does not initiate automated calls about specific purchases or request account resolution via unsolicited robocall.

### PG&E Utility Shutoff Threats

This scam is highly specific to Central California, where **Pacific Gas & Electric (PG&E)** is the primary utility provider for most 559 communities. Scammers spoofing local numbers call residents and businesses claiming to be PG&E employees who have flagged the account for non-payment, and that power will be shut off within hours unless immediate payment is made via gift card or wire transfer. PG&E's legitimate disconnection process involves written notices sent well in advance.

### Medicare "Final Notice" Scams

Scammers targeting the region's senior population use spoofed local numbers to call Medicare beneficiaries, identifying themselves as "patient advocates" and claiming it is the recipient's "final notice" before their Medicare eligibility is suspended. Real Medicare representatives do not call unsolicited claiming imminent suspension of benefits.

### Social Security Administration Impersonation

Voicemails from spoofed local numbers claim to be from the SSA, stating that "suspicious activity" has been detected on the recipient's Social Security Number and that legal action or account suspension is imminent. The SSA does not leave threatening voicemails demanding immediate callback to avoid legal consequences.

### Law Enforcement Impersonation

Scammers have specifically impersonated the **Fresno County Sheriff's Office**, calling residents to claim they have a warrant and must pay a fine or appear in court immediately. The Fresno County Sheriff has publicly stated that its office does not contact people by phone to solicit money or payment of fines.

### Apple iCloud/iTunes Breach Scams

Robocalls claiming to be from Apple Support assert that the recipient's iCloud or iTunes account has been compromised, directing them to press 1 or call a toll-free number. Apple does not initiate unsolicited calls to users about account security.

### Staying Protected

- Never pay a fine, provide account information, or allow remote access to your device in response to an unsolicited call claiming to be PG&E, Medicare, the SSA, law enforcement, Amazon, or Apple.
- If a caller claims to represent a utility or government agency, hang up and call the published official number directly to verify.
- Businesses operating legitimate local lines benefit from **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP providers, which verify caller ID at the carrier level and help recipients distinguish authentic calls from spoofed fraud attempts.
- Report suspicious calls at reportfraud.ftc.gov or to the Fresno County Sheriff.
