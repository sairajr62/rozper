## What Is the 213 Area Code?

![Downtown Los Angeles Skyline and Business District](/images/blog/213-image-1.webp)

The **213 area code** is one of the most historically significant telephone codes in North America. When the North American Numbering Plan (NANP) launched in **1947**, 213 was assigned to cover the entire state of California — every city from San Diego to San Francisco operated under a single 213 code. As California's population exploded through the second half of the 20th century, the state was subdivided repeatedly, leaving 213 as the inherited code of Los Angeles's core — the downtown district and its immediately surrounding neighborhoods.

Today, 213 operates as part of a three-code overlay with **323** and **738**, all serving the same geographic territory in and around central Los Angeles.

## History of the 213 Area Code

![213 323 and 738 Overlay Code History Diagram](/images/blog/213-image-2.webp)

The **213 area code** holds a distinction that no other California code can claim: it was the original. In 1947, when the Bell System introduced the North American Numbering Plan, California was assigned a single area code — **213** — to cover the entire state. In the seven decades that followed, that single code would be split five times to accommodate one of the fastest-growing telecommunication markets in the world.

For decades, area code 213 served the entire state of California before being progressively refined through a series of splits as population and telephone demand surged through the postwar boom.

### The Five Splits of 213

**714 (1951):** The first split carved out Southern California — Orange County and the Inland Empire — as postwar suburban development pushed the region's phone demand past what a single code could handle.

**805 (1957):** Ventura, Santa Barbara, and the Central Coast split off to form 805, stripping another segment of the original statewide footprint.

**818 (1984):** The San Fernando Valley — Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and the suburbs north of the Hollywood Hills — was reassigned to 818, removing one of the most populous suburban zones.

**310 (1991):** The Westside and South Bay — Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, El Segundo, Torrance, and the coastal cities — moved to 310. This is the same territory where the [424 area code](/blog/424-area-code) later became an overlay, extending number capacity for the Westside alongside 310.

**323 (1998):** The fifth and final split separated Hollywood, East LA, and Southeast LA County cities from 213, creating the **323 area code**. After this split, 213 served only Downtown Los Angeles and a narrow band of immediately adjacent neighborhoods.

### 2017: The 213/323 Recombination as an Overlay

By 2017, the pace of new number demand from mobile devices, VoIP lines, and IoT services was pushing both 213 and 323 toward exhaustion. Rather than a sixth split — which would have required massive customer number changes — the **California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC)** eliminated the geographic boundary between 213 and 323.

Effective **July 8, 2017**, 213 and 323 became a full overlay. Telephone companies could assign any available 213 numbers in the former 323 zone and vice versa. The operational consequence for all subscribers was mandatory **10-digit dialing** for every local call across the entire overlay territory.

### 2024: The 738 Area Code Added

As mobile, VoIP, and connected device growth continued, a third code was needed. The CPUC approved **738** as a new overlay on **March 16, 2023**. Starting **November 1, 2024**, new subscribers in the 213/323 region began receiving 738 numbers. The 738 addition changed nothing operationally — 10-digit dialing was already mandatory, and no existing numbers changed.

## 213 Area Code Time Zone

![Pacific Standard Time and Daylight Time Zone Chart](/images/blog/213-image-4.webp)

The 213/323/738 zone operates in the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**:

- **Pacific Standard Time (PST):** UTC−8 — observed from early November to mid-March
- **Pacific Daylight Time (PDT):** UTC−7 — observed from mid-March to early November

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**East Coast calls (ET → PT):** New York and the Eastern Time Zone are 3 hours ahead of Los Angeles. A Downtown LA business receiving a 9 AM ET call gets it at 6 AM PT — before business hours. Morning calls to East Coast clients should go out before 3 PM PT to reach them before 6 PM ET.

**Central Time (CT → PT):** Chicago and Central Time are 2 hours ahead. A noon CT call reaches LA at 10 AM PT — comfortable business hours for both.

**Mountain Time (MT → PT):** MT is 1 hour ahead. Note that Arizona (outside Navajo Nation) does not observe Daylight Saving Time, which can create a 2-hour gap during DST periods.

**International calls to 213:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 213 + 7-digit number. From Australia, dial 0011 + 1 + 213 + 7-digit number. Globally via mobile: +1-213-XXX-XXXX.

Because 213, 310, and 424 all share Pacific Time, businesses managing calls across all three LA-area codes face no time zone complexity within the metro.

## Is a 213 Area Code Call a Scam?

The 213 prefix is legitimate — it covers real businesses and residents throughout Downtown LA and Central Los Angeles. However, it is also the **#1 reported code for scam calls in California**, with the FTC logging **49,476 complaints** from thousands of unique phone numbers using spoofed caller IDs. The August of the most recent reporting year saw the highest single-month complaint volume.

Scammers prefer local LA numbers for a direct reason: in California, a familiar prefix signals Los Angeles — and local calls get answered at far higher rates than out-of-state or toll-free numbers.

### Fake Job Offer Scams

One of the most prevalent scam types targeting this code involves fraudulent employment offers, particularly aimed at recent graduates and job seekers in LA's entertainment, tech, and media industries. Victims receive a text or call offering an audition, a content creation contract, or a remote role, then are asked to pay for background checks, equipment, or training materials to proceed. No legitimate employer requests upfront payment as part of a hiring process.

### Text Message Scams ("Conversational" Style)

The FTC and consumer reporting platforms have flagged a high volume of text scams that open with casual, personal-seeming messages: "Hello," "Are you busy?", "Sup, didn't we meet at the conference?" These are engineered to start a conversation that gradually steers toward a request for money, personal information, or cryptocurrency. If a text from an unknown local number appears personal but you cannot place the contact, do not engage.

### IRS and Government Impersonation

Callers spoofing local numbers claim to be IRS agents, Social Security Administration representatives, or Homeland Security officers. They assert that a warrant or account freeze is imminent unless immediate payment is made via wire transfer, gift card, or cryptocurrency. The IRS does not initiate contact by phone call, and no real government agency demands immediate cryptocurrency payment.

### Tech Support Fraud

Victims receive a call or text from a "Microsoft," "Apple," or "Google" support agent claiming their device has been compromised. They are directed to a remote access tool that gives the scammer control of their screen and banking applications. Legitimate technology companies do not call consumers to report device infections.

### Staying Protected

- Never send money, provide account numbers, or install remote access software based on an unsolicited call or text from an unfamiliar LA number.
- Legitimate employers, government agencies, and tech companies do not demand upfront payment by phone.
- If you run a business with an LA phone number, using a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider helps your callers see your number as verified rather than potentially spoofed — important in a market where scam rates are the highest in the state.
- Report suspicious calls and texts at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
