## History of the 228 Area Code

When the North American Numbering Plan (NANP) launched in 1947, Mississippi was assigned a single area code — **601** — to cover the entire state. For five decades, every home, business, and payphone in Mississippi dialed under the same 601 prefix. As the state's population grew and the proliferation of fax machines, pagers, and early mobile phones accelerated demand for new numbers, the 601 numbering supply began approaching exhaustion.

### The 1997 Split: 228 Created

Area code 228 was split from 601 on **September 15, 1997**. Mississippi's Gulf Coast counties were carved out to form the new code, with the three southernmost counties — Hancock, Harrison, and Jackson — becoming 228, while the rest of the state retained 601.

Two years later, in 1999, the northern half of Mississippi was further split from 601 to create **662**, giving the state three area codes aligned with its three geographic regions: northern (662), central/southern (601), and Gulf Coast (228).

### No Overlay — 228 Remains Sole Code

Unlike many high-growth metro codes, the **228 area code** has never required an overlay. No second code has been added to the region. This means that — unlike the [213 area code](/blog/213-area-code) in Los Angeles, which now operates alongside 323 and 738 — every phone number on the Mississippi Gulf Coast carries the same 228 prefix. For businesses, this simplifies branding: a 228 number is unambiguously identified as a Gulf Coast Mississippi number with no competing local codes.

## 228 Area Code Time Zone

![Central Time Zone Best Calling Hours for Gulf Coast Businesses](/images/blog/228-image-3.webp)

The 228 zone operates in the **Central Time Zone (CT)**:

- **Central Standard Time (CST):** UTC−6 — observed from early November to mid-March
- **Central Daylight Time (CDT):** UTC−5 — observed from mid-March to early November

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**To/from the East Coast (ET):** Eastern Time is 1 hour ahead of Central. A Gulf Coast business calling a New York or Atlanta client at 9 AM CT reaches them at 10 AM ET — ideal overlap. East Coast clients calling the 228 zone in the late afternoon (5 PM ET) reach the Gulf Coast at 4 PM CT — still within business hours.

**To/from the West Coast (PT):** Pacific Time is 2 hours behind Central. Calling Los Angeles or Seattle from Gulfport at 9 AM CT reaches the recipient at 7 AM PT — too early. Best practice: wait until after 11 AM CT (9 AM PT) for first-call outreach to West Coast contacts.

**To/from Mountain Time (MT):** MT is 1 hour behind CT. A 10 AM CT call reaches Mountain Time at 9 AM — easy coordination. Note Arizona's no-DST exception during summer months.

**International calls to 228:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 228 + 7-digit number. From Australia, dial 0011 + 1 + 228 + 7-digit number. On mobile: +1-228-XXX-XXXX.

The Central Time Zone positions Gulf Coast businesses comfortably between the two largest US time zones — able to reach East Coast clients in the morning and West Coast clients in the afternoon within a standard working day.

## Is a 228 Area Code Call a Scam?

The 228 prefix is legitimate — it serves real residents and businesses across Mississippi's Gulf Coast. Like all US area codes, however, it is subject to caller ID spoofing by scammers who display local numbers to increase answer rates in the region.

Consumer reporting data identifies several high-frequency scam patterns on 228 numbers:

### Health Insurance Robocalls

The most commonly reported scam type involves prerecorded calls claiming to offer subsidized or "government" health insurance plans. These calls target uninsured and underinsured residents — a demographic present in Mississippi at above-national-average rates — and direct victims to provide personal and financial details in exchange for quote information. Legitimate health insurance providers and ACA marketplace navigators do not cold-call offering coverage without an existing inquiry.

### Auto Warranty Fraud

Auto warranty robocalls using spoofed 228 numbers claim that the recipient's vehicle warranty is expiring and require immediate payment for an extension. These calls generate a high volume of complaints across the Gulf Coast because the region has a higher-than-average rate of vehicle ownership due to limited public transit infrastructure. Genuine dealership warranty departments contact customers in writing, not by unsolicited robocall.

### Medicare and Medicaid Impersonation

Given Mississippi's older coastal population and high Medicaid enrollment rate, scammers frequently spoof local numbers to impersonate CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) agents. They claim that a new card, an updated benefit, or a required verification must be processed immediately. Real CMS and Social Security Administration agents do not call requesting account numbers or payment.

### Amazon Order and Delivery Fraud

Scammers impersonate Amazon customer service using spoofed caller IDs, claiming an unauthorized purchase was made on the recipient's account. They request account credentials or gift card payments to "resolve" the fraudulent charge. Amazon does not initiate inbound calls to customers about specific order issues.

### Social Security Number Suspension

Calls claiming that the recipient's Social Security number has been "suspended" due to suspicious activity are widely reported on spoofed Gulf Coast numbers. No government agency suspends Social Security numbers, and legitimate enforcement actions are not initiated through unsolicited phone calls.

### Staying Protected

- If a call from a 228 number you don't recognize offers health insurance, a warranty extension, or claims a government benefit is at risk, hang up and call the agency's published number directly to verify.
- Businesses with local Gulf Coast numbers benefit from using a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider — this framework verifies caller ID at the carrier level.
- Report suspicious calls at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
