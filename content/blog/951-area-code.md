# 951 Area Code: A Complete Guide to California's Inland Empire

The Inland Empire is one of Southern California's fastest-growing economic regions — a logistics, manufacturing, and small-business engine sitting just east of Los Angeles. And across most of its western half, business runs on one set of digits: the **951 area code**. For any company trying to reach customers in Riverside, Corona, or Temecula, a 951 number is a quiet signal that says "we're local here."

This guide breaks down where 951 sits on the map, the cities and commercial hubs it covers, how it split off from 909, the time zone it follows, and the fastest way to get a 951 number of your own.

## Where Is the 951 Area Code?

![Understanding the Inland Empire Code — Geographic Footprint and Key Economic Zones](/images/blog/951-image-1.webp)

The **951 area code** serves western Riverside County in Southern California, along with a small slice of San Bernardino County. Geographically, it sits in the heart of the Inland Empire — the inland region east of Los Angeles and north of San Diego that has grown rapidly as people and businesses move away from the coastal price crunch.

Unlike many California area codes, the **951 area code** has no overlay. It's the single code across its western Riverside County footprint, which means **seven-digit local dialing still works** within the region — a small convenience that overlay markets like Los Angeles lost long ago.

## Key Cities in the 951 Area Code

The **951 area code** covers a string of cities that together form a major commercial corridor. A few stand out as anchors of the local economy:

- **Riverside** — the county seat and commercial core, home to government, healthcare, and the University of California, Riverside.
- **Moreno Valley** — one of the region's most rapidly growing cities and a key logistics and distribution market.
- **Corona** — a business gateway on the Orange County border with a growing base of tech and manufacturing firms.
- **Temecula** — wine country and a tourism-driven economy that draws visitors from across the Southwest.

Beyond those four, the **951 area code** also reaches Murrieta, Hemet, Menifee, Perris, Lake Elsinore, Eastvale, San Jacinto, Beaumont, Wildomar, and Banning, among others.

## From 909 to 951: A Short History

The **951 area code** is relatively young. It went into service on July 17, 2004, created by splitting it off from the older 909 code. By the early 2000s, the Inland Empire's rapid growth had drained the supply of available 909 numbers, so regulators carved western Riverside County into its own numbering plan with the new **area code 951** prefix.

Since then, **area code 951** has held its own without needing an overlay or a further split — a sign the original plan gave the region enough room to grow.

## What Time Zone Is the 951 Area Code In?

The entire **951 area code** region runs on Pacific Time. That's Pacific Standard Time (UTC−8) in the winter months and Pacific Daylight Time (UTC−7) during daylight saving. If you're scheduling calls or support hours for a 951 number, treat it as Pacific Time — three hours behind the East Coast, and the same clock as Los Angeles and San Diego.

## Is the 951 Area Code a Scam?

A 951 number showing up on caller ID isn't a red flag on its own. The **951 area code** is a legitimate, geographically assigned Southern California code tied to a real, populous region.

The catch — true of every area code — is spoofing. Scammers can fake a local-looking 951 number to boost the odds you'll pick up. So the prefix only tells you the call claims to come from western Riverside County; it says nothing about the caller's intent. The safer habit is to judge the specific number and let unfamiliar calls go to voicemail, rather than trusting or distrusting an area code outright.

The [949 area code](/blog/949-area-code) covers neighboring Orange County — the communities just west of Corona — making it another regional code to recognize if you receive calls from across that county line.
