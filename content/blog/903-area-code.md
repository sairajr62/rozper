## Where Is the 903?

The **903 area code** serves Northeast Texas. This region stretches from the eastern edge of Dallas-Fort Worth to the Arkansas and Louisiana state lines. It split from area code 214 in 1990, when the Dallas metro outgrew one prefix. That split gave the region its own name. It still holds true today.

The coverage zone covers mid-sized cities, rural counties, and towns with deep farming and energy roots. The 214, 469, and 972 codes define DFW. The 903 code stands for a different Texas. Here, community ties and local ties carry real weight.

The [469 area code](/blog/469-area-code) anchors businesses in the Dallas-Fort Worth area. In the same way, the **903 area code** anchors businesses across Northeast Texas. This region has its own economy.

## Protect Your 903 Number: Easy Ways to Avoid Scam Calls

Phone scams that target local area codes have grown a lot. Fraudsters use 903 numbers to look like familiar local callers. This trick is called neighbor spoofing. Why? Because local numbers get answered more often than out-of-state ones.

![Protect Your 903 Number](/images/blog/903-image-3.webp)

### Common Phone Scams in the 903 Area Code Region

**Spoofing:** Scammers hide their real number behind a 903 prefix, so the call looks local. You see what looks like a neighbor, a local business, or a government office. So you answer. Then the scam starts: fake IRS agents, threats to shut off your utilities, or fake prize offers.

**Fake government calls:** Fraudsters pretend to be the Social Security Administration, the IRS, or local offices. This is common in rural Northeast Texas. They use 903 numbers to make the "local official" trick feel more real.

**Business directory scams:** Callers claim they need to "update" or "verify" your business listing. Then they charge you a fee for a listing that doesn't even exist.

### How to Protect Yourself

- **Verify before acting:** Hang up. Then call them back using a number from their official website. Do not use the number that called you.
- **Register with the Do Not Call Registry:** Sign up free at the [national Do Not Call registry](https://www.donotcall.gov/) to cut down on unwanted sales calls.
- **Use call screening tools:** Most carriers offer spam call filters. Turn one on.
- **Report suspicious calls:** File a complaint with the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and the FCC at consumercomplaints.fcc.gov.
- **Train your team:** Staff should check who is calling first. Do this before you share info or handle any request.

Scammers who misuse a prefix can still hurt real businesses that share it. A verified, clean presence tells customers your number is safe to trust.
