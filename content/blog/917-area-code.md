## What Is the 917 Area Code and Where Does It Apply?

The 917 area code is a citywide overlay for New York City. Unlike area codes that serve a single county or metro district, 917 covers all five boroughs simultaneously — Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, The Bronx, and Staten Island — alongside the other NYC overlay codes (212, 332, 646, 718, 347, and 929).

When 917 launched in 1992, it was assigned exclusively to cellular phones and pagers, making it the first code in the US primarily tied to mobile communications. That early mobile identity gives 917 numbers a particular resonance with business contacts — they read as active, reachable, and metropolitan.

**Time zone:** Eastern Time (ET) — UTC−5 (November to March) and UTC−4 during Daylight Saving Time (March to November). If you're calling or messaging 917 contacts from the West Coast or internationally, factor in the 3-hour difference from Pacific time and the 5-hour difference from UTC.

**Area code history at a glance:**
- 1947 — Original 212 code assigned to all of New York State
- 1984 — 718 split to serve New York City's outer boroughs
- 1992 — 917 introduced as the first NANP overlay code (cellular/pager only)
- 1999 — 646 and 347 added as Manhattan and outer borough overlays
- 2011 — 929 added for outer boroughs; 917 extended to landlines
- 2026 — Area code 465 approved by the New York PSC, in-service date June 18, 2026, as existing codes approach exhaustion

The upcoming 465 overlay — the first NYC area code beginning with a 4 — signals just how saturated the market has become. The existing codes are projected to exhaust in Q4 2026, after which 465 will carry new number assignments for an estimated 11 years. If establishing a 917 number is on your roadmap, now is the time.
