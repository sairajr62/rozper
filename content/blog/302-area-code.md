## Phone Scam Alert: Securing Yourself Against Local Number Fraud

While a local area code like 302 is excellent for building trust and a local presence, it's also a tool that scammers can unfortunately exploit through "spoofing." Spoofing is when a scammer fakes their caller ID to make it look like they are calling from a local number, like one with the 302 area code, or even a number very similar to your own.

![Phone Scam Alert Securing Yourself Against Local Number Fraud](/images/blog/302-scam-alert.webp)


### Common Scams Using Local-Like Numbers

Scammers use spoofed local numbers for various fraudulent activities. Being aware of these common tactics is the first step in protecting yourself:

- **Impersonation Scams:** Callers may pretend to be from government agencies, utility companies, or even well-known tech companies. They often create a sense of urgency, threatening penalties or offering fake refunds to get your personal information or money.
- **Phishing for Information (Vishing):** Voice phishing, or "vishing," involves scammers trying to trick you into revealing sensitive personal or financial details, such as bank account numbers, credit card details, passwords, or your Social Security number. They might claim there's a problem with your account or a suspicious transaction.
- **'One-Ring' or 'Wangiri' Scams:** While often associated with international numbers, variations can occur. Your phone rings once from an unknown (potentially spoofed local) number. The scammer hopes you'll call back, and if the number is routed to a premium-rate service, you could incur high charges.
- **Tech Support Scams:** You might receive an unsolicited call from someone claiming to be from a major tech company, stating your computer has a virus or other issue. They'll try to gain remote access to your device or sell you unnecessary and often fake services.
- **Fake Prize or Lottery Winnings:** A caller informs you that you've won a significant prize or lottery, but you need to pay a fee or provide personal information to claim it. Legitimate lotteries don't ask for money upfront to claim a prize.

### Protecting Yourself from Phone Scams

Vigilance and proactive measures can significantly reduce your risk of falling victim to phone scams, even when the caller ID looks local:

- **Be Skeptical of Unsolicited Calls:** If you receive an unexpected call from a number you don't recognize, let it go to voicemail. If it's important, the caller will leave a message. Scammers rarely do.
- **Verify the Caller Independently:** If a caller claims to be from a known organization (bank, utility, government agency), hang up. Find the official contact number for that organization from their website or a statement and call them directly to verify the claim. Do not use any callback numbers provided by the unsolicited caller.
- **Never Share Personal Information:** Do not provide or confirm personal, financial, or sensitive information like your Social Security number, bank account details, credit card numbers, or passwords over the phone unless you initiated the call and are certain you are speaking to a legitimate representative.
- **Don't Trust Caller ID Alone:** Remember that caller ID can be faked. Just because it shows a local 302 number or a familiar company name doesn't guarantee the call is legitimate.
- **Register on the National Do Not Call Registry:** While this won't stop illegal scam calls (as scammers don't respect the list), it can help reduce legitimate telemarketing calls. You can register at [donotcall.gov](https://www.donotcall.gov/).
- **Report Scam Calls:** Report suspicious calls to the Federal Trade Commission. You can also report them to the Delaware Attorney General's Consumer Protection Unit. Providing details helps authorities track and combat scammers.
