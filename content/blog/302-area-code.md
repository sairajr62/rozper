# 302 Area Code: A Complete Guide to Delaware's Statewide Number

Most states juggle a handful of area codes. Delaware uses exactly one. Dial any landline or cell phone from Wilmington in the north to the beach towns in the south, and the number starts the same way: 302. That rare "one state, one code" status makes the **302 area code** a genuine piece of Delaware identity — and a smart number to own if you do business in the First State. ## The 302 Area Code Covers the Entire First State

There's no boundary line to memorize with the **302 area code**, because it serves all of Delaware. Every one of the state's three counties — New Castle in the north, Kent in the middle, and Sussex along the southern coast — falls under 302. From the corporate offices of Wilmington to the boardwalk of Rehoboth Beach, it's the same three digits.

That makes Delaware one of a small group of U.S. states served by a single area code. For a state this compact, one code has been enough for decades, and there are no plans to change that arrangement anytime soon.

## One of America's Original Area Codes

The **302 area code** isn't just Delaware's only code — it's one of the oldest in the country. It was assigned in 1947 as part of the very first wave of North American Numbering Plan area codes, administered since the founding era of American telephony — a testament to the [State of Delaware](https://delaware.gov/)'s enduring commercial heritage as the First State. Many regions have since been split or layered with overlay codes as phone numbers ran short, but Delaware never needed to make that change.

Since 1947, **area code 302** has served the state without a single split or overlay across more than 75 years of continuous use. That longevity is part of why "302" has become shorthand for Delaware itself, showing up on apparel, local brands, and community pride well beyond the phone network. The single-code setup also means **seven-digit dialing still works** for local calls within the state — a small convenience that overlay regions lost years ago.

## What Time Zone Is the 302 Area Code In?

All of Delaware, and therefore the entire **302 area code**, sits in the Eastern Time Zone. That's Eastern Standard Time (UTC−5) in the colder months and Eastern Daylight Time (UTC−4) during daylight saving. If you're scheduling calls or support coverage for a 302 number, treat it as Eastern Time — the same clock as Philadelphia, New York, and Washington, D.C.

Knowing the time zone matters most when your team is distributed. A sales representative in California calling a Delaware prospect should plan for a three-hour difference during standard hours.

## Is a 302 Call Safe? Scams and Spoofing

A common worry: "I got a call from a 302 number I don't recognize — is it a scam?" The honest answer is that the **302 area code** itself is completely legitimate — it's the official, decades-old code for Delaware. What muddies the water is spoofing, where scammers fake a local-looking **area code 302** number to raise the odds you'll answer.

The prefix only tells you the call claims to originate in Delaware — nothing more. The safer habit is to evaluate the specific caller and let unknown numbers go to voicemail. If you receive repeated unwanted calls from a 302 number, you can report it to the Federal Trade Commission and to your phone carrier's fraud team.

## Landline, Mobile, or VoIP?

The **302 area code** isn't reserved for any one type of line. It's assigned across residential landlines, mobile phones, businesses, government offices, and modern VoIP services. So a 302 number could be a home phone in Dover, a cell in Newark, or a cloud-based business line routed from another state entirely.

Maryland's telephone geography offers an instructive comparison: the 240 area code operates as an overlay alongside 301 across much of Maryland, meaning callers in the same area may carry either prefix. Understanding [the 240 area code](/blog/240-area-code) and how overlays work is a useful contrast to Delaware's lean single-code system, which has kept things simple for residents and businesses for nearly eight decades without requiring ten-digit dialing for local calls.
