## What Is the 615 Area Code?

![Map graphic explaining the origins of Nashville's telephone prefix](/images/blog/What Is the 615 Area Code.webp)

The **615 area code** is one of the first North American Numbering Plan codes. It began in 1947 to serve central Tennessee. For decades, it was the main prefix for Nashville and the counties near it. Over time, the region grew. It went from a small hub into a national center for music, health care, and finance.

Today, this prefix works with the 629 overlay. That overlay started in 2014. It added more phone numbers in the same area. The two codes cover the exact same map area. Together they act as one local calling area. So every call in the region needs the full 10-digit dial. Dialing just seven digits will not connect a call in the 615/629 area.

The region runs on **Central Time (CT)**. That is UTC-6 in the winter months, from early November through mid-March. It is UTC-5 in the summer months, from mid-March through early November. Businesses in Eastern, Mountain, or Pacific time zones should plan for this gap. This matters when you set up calls with Nashville or Murfreesboro contacts.

## History of Area Code 615

![Timeline tracing Nashville's prefix from 1947 to the 629 overlay](/images/blog/History of Area Code 615.webp)

**Area code 615** was one of the first 86 codes given out when the North American Numbering Plan launched in 1947. At first, it covered a large part of Tennessee. Later splits shrank its map to just the central region.

Key facts that define the 615 territory:

- **1947:** The 615 prefix was established as one of the original NANP codes serving central Tennessee
- **1995–1996:** Subdivisions reduced 615 to the Nashville and middle Tennessee region as new codes were carved out
- **2014:** The 629 overlay was introduced across the same territory, cementing mandatory 10-digit dialing

When this prefix was first given out, Nashville was a small regional center. It was known mainly for music publishing and state government. Today it ranks among the fastest-growing city economies in the country. Big health care firms, a busy tourism trade, and a growing tech scene now anchor its suburbs. The [FCC's consumer dialing guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/ten-digit-dialing) posts federal rules on overlays and 10-digit dialing. It is the top source for how these rules work nationwide. Rutherford and Williamson counties have grown fast since the 1990s. Because of this, each new numbering plan update has had to handle demand that beat past guesses. This pattern is common in fast-growing Sun Belt cities.

## FAQ'S

**What cities does the 615 code serve?**
The **615 area code** covers Nashville and north-central Tennessee. This includes Murfreesboro, Franklin, Hendersonville, Gallatin, Mount Juliet, and Smyrna. These sit across Davidson, Rutherford, Williamson, Sumner, and Wilson counties. It shares this area with the 629 overlay. Together, the two codes give middle Tennessee strong, shared local ties.

**What is the difference between 615 and 629?**
Both codes cover the exact same map area as overlay partners. Folks across middle Tennessee see both as local. 615 is the first code, dating to 1947. **Area code 629** was added in 2014 as an overlay to meet growing demand for new numbers. To callers, a 615 number feels just as local as a 629 number anywhere in the shared area.

**Do I need a Tennessee address to get a 615 number?**
No. VoIP providers let businesses anywhere set up and run a local Nashville number. You do not need a real Tennessee address or office. **What time zone is the 615 code in?**
The 615 code runs on Central Time (CT). That is UTC-6 during standard time and UTC-5 during daylight saving time. Businesses in other time zones should plan Nashville outreach during Central business hours, roughly 8 AM to 6 PM CT. This gives you the best chance of reaching contacts in Nashville, Franklin, and beyond while they are at their desk.

**Is 10-digit dialing required for the 615 code?**
Yes. Because 615 shares its area with the 629 overlay, every call in the region needs the full 10-digit number. Dialing only seven digits leads to a failed call. Businesses should set up all phone systems, CRMs, and dial-out strings to use the full 10-digit format across the entire 615/629 service area.
