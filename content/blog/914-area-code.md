## What Is the 914 Area Code?

The 914 area code covers Westchester County, New York. It sits just north of the Bronx. This code has served the area since 1947. That makes it one of the original area codes in the whole country. The North American numbering plan launched that year. Area code 914 was part of the very first batch. Very few codes from that first group are still active today. This one is.

Most codes near big cities eventually need an overlay. An overlay adds a second code to the same region. Area code 914 has never needed one. Instead, its territory shrank over time. Nearby regions split off and got their own codes. What's left is a smaller, stable area. It still doesn't share its numbers with a second code.

Westchester's economy runs on a few strong industries. Corporate headquarters anchor much of the county. Major hospital networks employ thousands of local workers. Suburban retail centers serve residents throughout the region. Most of this activity clusters along the Hudson River. The I-287 corridor carries its own heavy share too. Together, these industries create steady demand for phone lines. Local businesses need lines that work and don't drop calls.

## How This New York Prefix Came to Be

The 914 area code first went into service in 1947. It was one of the original 86 area codes ever created. Back then, the territory covered far more than Westchester. It stretched across all of Long Island and deep into the Lower Hudson Valley to the north.

That huge original footprint shrank in two major stages:

- **1951**: Nassau and Suffolk counties on Long Island split off, taking the [516 area code](/blog/516-area-code) with them.
- **2000**: The Lower Hudson Valley counties north of Westchester were reassigned the 845 area code, narrowing area code 914 down to Westchester County alone.

Those two splits explain a lot. The modern 914 territory is a small fraction of its 1947 size. Yet the wider metro area it links to still holds a population well over four million. This code has never needed an overlay to keep pace.

## Time Zone and Dialing Rules for This Prefix

Every 914 number runs on Eastern Time. That means Eastern Standard Time, or EST, in the winter. It shifts to Eastern Daylight Time, or EDT, from spring through fall. EST sits at UTC-5. EDT sits at UTC-4. Westchester runs three hours ahead of the West Coast.

This region has no overlay in place. That means seven-digit dialing still works for local calls. You just dial the last seven digits within the same prefix. Many neighboring regions have already lost this option. They now require ten digits for every local call. Calling Westchester from outside the region? Dial all ten digits anyway. That's the only sure way to route the call.

Here's a quick example for planning calls across time zones. A 9:00 a.m. meeting in White Plains lands at 6:00 a.m. Pacific time. That same meeting falls at 2:00 p.m. in London. Double-checking the time zone before you dial saves everyone a headache. It also helps teams avoid missed calls with Westchester partners.
