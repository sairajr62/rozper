# The 914 Area Code: Westchester County's Enduring Local Identity

A call from this prefix has carried a distinctly Westchester signature since the very first days of the North American Numbering Plan, even though the territory behind those three digits has shrunk considerably since then. What began as coverage for nearly a dozen counties has narrowed into one of the most recognizable suburban New York identities on its own. Whether you just moved to the area, launched a company nearby, or are simply curious about the number on your caller ID, this guide walks through where the prefix reaches, how it came to be, and why it still matters for business today.

## The Westchester Communities a 914 Area Code Number Reaches

The 914 area code covers all of Westchester County, New York, reaching Yonkers, White Plains, New Rochelle, Mount Vernon, Peekskill, Port Chester, Rye, Tarrytown, Scarsdale, and Ossining. The territory stretches from the Hudson River waterfront communities out through Mamaroneck, Larchmont, Harrison, Armonk, Chappaqua, Mount Kisco, and Yorktown Heights, tying together one of the most affluent suburban economies just north of New York City. A number under this prefix signals a genuine connection to the river towns, business parks, and commuter suburbs that define greater Westchester.

Westchester County blends a dense concentration of corporate headquarters and finance offices with growing healthcare, education, and biotech sectors, and the steady flow of commuters and relocating businesses keeps demand for local phone numbers unusually high. That mix of steady population and constant business turnover is part of why this particular prefix has stayed in heavy demand since it was introduced.

Westchester County Airport and the region's dense commuter rail network both add a constant stream of business travelers and professionals who interact with local companies daily, which keeps demand for a recognizable prefix elevated even compared with other New York City suburbs.

The county's mix of riverfront towns, corporate parks, and quiet residential villages means a single local number can represent a business with customers spread across a genuinely diverse footprint rather than one narrow slice of the suburbs.

Some of these communities, like White Plains and Tarrytown, have grown into dense commercial hubs over the past two decades, while others, like Bronxville and Pleasantville, have kept much of their small-village character even as the broader county has modernized around them.

Companies researching numbering options nearby may also want to know that the neighboring 845 area code covers the Hudson Valley counties just north of Westchester, since both prefixes now share a single overlay complex under the current numbering plan.

## From a Nine-County Original to Westchester's Own Code

![Timeline graphic showing the 914 area code's history from a nine-county original to Westchester's own code](/images/blog/from-a-nine-county-original-to-westchesters-own-code.webp)

Area code 914 was one of the original 86 numbering plan areas created in 1947, when it covered an enormous swath of downstate New York including Delaware, Dutchess, Nassau, Orange, Putnam, Rockland, Suffolk, Sullivan, Ulster, and Westchester Counties. Decades of population growth steadily shrank that footprint as the region was divided into additional codes.

In 1951, regulators split off area code 516 to cover Nassau and Suffolk Counties on Long Island, and by 2000, growth in the remaining territory forced another split. Regulators created area code 845 on June 5, 2000, to cover the rest of the lower Hudson Valley, narrowing 914 down to Westchester County alone.

More recently, regulators introduced area code 329 as an overlay spanning both the 845 and 914 territories across ten Hudson Valley counties, meaning existing customers kept their 914 numbers while new lines could be issued under the new code.

## Eastern Time and Ten-Digit Dialing in the Hudson Valley

![Graphic explaining Eastern Time and ten-digit dialing rules in the Hudson Valley](/images/blog/eastern-time-and-ten-digit-dialing-in-the-hudson-valley.webp)

Every community inside the 914 area code operates on Eastern Time, observing Eastern Standard Time in winter and Eastern Daylight Time for the rest of the year. That places Westchester in step with New York City, Boston, and most of the East Coast for scheduling purposes.

Ten-digit dialing became mandatory across the 914 territory on October 24, 2021, and it remains standard practice now that the region shares its numbering pool with the newer 329 overlay. For a deeper look at how this numbering plan evolved, [Wikipedia's entry on area code 914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_code_914) lays out a well-documented timeline of the splits and the later overlay. Getting used to dialing all ten digits is a minor adjustment, but it remains essential for anyone routing calls into or out of Westchester today.
