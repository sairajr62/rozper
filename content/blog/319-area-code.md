## What Is the 319 Area Code?

![Map graphic highlighting the eastern and central Iowa counties served by the 319 prefix](/images/blog/What Is the 319 Area Code.webp)

The **319 area code** started in 1981. It split off from the old 515 code, which once served all of Iowa alone. Iowa's people and business grew a lot. So the state needed a new code for the eastern and central part. That split made 319.

Today, 319 stands on its own. It covers 12 counties across eastern and central Iowa. Many big metro areas now share one region with a second, overlapping code. This is called an overlay. But 319 has no overlay. Every new number here still starts with 319. This keeps a strong local feel and avoids mix-ups. It also helps people spot a real local call right away. Every 319 number, old or new, ties back to the same home turf.

The region runs on **Central Time (CT)**. That's UTC-6 in standard time, from early November through mid-March. It's UTC-5 in daylight saving time, from mid-March through early November. If your business sits in the Eastern or Pacific time zone, plan around this gap. This helps when you call Cedar Rapids or Iowa City contacts.

## History of Area Code 319

![Timeline graphic showing the 1981 split from area code 515 that created the 319 prefix](/images/blog/History of Area Code 319.webp)

**Area code 319** was made in 1981. Iowa's growth made it necessary to split the state's old 515 code into two parts. Eastern and central Iowa got 319. Central and western Iowa, including Des Moines, kept 515.

Key milestones that shaped the 319 territory:

- **1981:** The 319 code began, serving eastern and central Iowa across 12 counties
- **2000:** Area code 563 split from 319 to serve northeastern Iowa, including Dubuque and the Quad Cities corridor
- **Present:** The code still stands as its own prefix. It serves its core territory — Cedar Rapids, Iowa City, Waterloo, and nearby counties

When the **319 area code** first began, Cedar Rapids was already Iowa's second-largest city. Today the Cedar Rapids metro area holds a mixed economy. It spans factories, finance, and farm-based services. A local phone number gives any new business here a real edge. ## FAQ'S

**What cities does the 319 code serve?**
This code covers 12 counties across eastern and central Iowa. That includes Cedar Rapids, Iowa City, Waterloo, Coralville, Marion, and Vinton. In 2000, area code 563 split off from 319. That move shifted northeastern Iowa, including Dubuque and the Quad Cities area, to the 563 code. This left 319 to serve the core eastern and central Iowa region.

**Does the 319 code have an overlay?**
No. This code runs on its own, with no overlay code sharing its area. Many metro regions use two or more codes at once to meet demand. But 319 keeps giving all new numbers the same 319 start. This keeps strong local recognition. It also skips the extra dialing steps that overlay systems can bring.

**Do I need an Iowa address to get a 319 number?**
No. VoIP providers let businesses anywhere get and run a local Iowa number. You don't need a real Iowa address or office. Calls route to any device your team uses — phone, computer, or desk phone — no matter where your staff sits.

**What time zone is the 319 code in?**
The 319 code runs on Central Time (CT): UTC-6 in standard time and UTC-5 in daylight saving time. If your business sits in another time zone, plan Iowa calls between about 8 AM and 6 PM CT. That window reaches Cedar Rapids and Iowa City contacts during their normal workday.

**What is the difference between 319 and 563?**
The 319 and 563 codes both serve Iowa, but they cover different areas. **Area code 319** covers eastern and central Iowa — Cedar Rapids, Iowa City, and Waterloo. Area code 563 covers northeastern Iowa, including Dubuque, Davenport, and the Quad Cities area along the Mississippi River. Both codes count as local calls within their own Iowa areas.
