## What Is the 510 Area Code? History, Coverage, and Cities

The 510 area code has deep roots in the East Bay's history. It was created on September 2, 1991, when the original 415 area code was split to accommodate the region's rapid population growth. In 1998, a second split created the 925 area code for the inland eastern portion of the East Bay. Then, in 2019, the 341 area code was introduced as an overlay to the 510 region to meet the ongoing demand for new numbers — making ten-digit dialing standard for all local calls. Any new number in the region may now carry either a 510 or 341 prefix.

### Geographic Coverage

The 510 area code primarily serves Alameda County and the western portions of Contra Costa County, covering one of the most economically diverse and densely populated corridors in California.

**Major Cities and Communities in the 510 Area Code:**

- **Oakland** — The largest city in Alameda County; a major port, cultural hub, and growing tech corridor
- **Berkeley** — Home to UC Berkeley and a globally recognized innovation ecosystem

- **Hayward** — A significant suburban and commercial hub
- **Richmond** — A key industrial city in western Contra Costa County
- **Alameda (city)** — An island city known for its maritime and commercial heritage
- **San Leandro** — An important commerce and industry hub
- **Union City** — A diverse community in the southern East Bay
- **Newark** — Part of the Tri-City area near Fremont
- **El Cerrito, San Pablo, Pinole, Hercules** — Communities in Contra Costa County

For businesses targeting this region, a 510 area code directly signals a connection to this economically vital and culturally rich territory.
