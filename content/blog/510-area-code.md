## What Is the 510 Area Code?

The 510 area code is a North American Numbering Plan (NANP) telephone area code serving the eastern shore of San Francisco Bay in California. It is one of the original area codes assigned when California's area code 415 was split to accommodate growing telephone demand in the region.

**Quick facts:**

- **State:** California
- **Region:** East Bay (eastern side of San Francisco Bay)
- **Time zone:** Pacific Time (PT) — UTC−8 standard, UTC−7 daylight saving
- **Overlay:** None currently active
- **Type:** Geographic landline and mobile

## History of the 510 Area Code

### 1947–1991: The 415 Era

From 1947 through 1991, the entire San Francisco Bay Area — including San Francisco, Marin County, the East Bay, and the North Bay — shared a single area code: **415**.

This worked fine for decades. But the explosive growth of the Bay Area through the 1970s and 1980s, driven by Silicon Valley's rise and population expansion, strained the 415 number pool significantly.

### 1991: The Split

On **August 3, 1991**, the 510 area code was officially carved out of 415. The East Bay — Alameda County and western Contra Costa County — received the new 510 designation. San Francisco, Marin, and the Peninsula retained 415.

This was one of California's first major area code splits and set a precedent for the dozens of splits that would follow across the state through the 1990s and 2000s.

### 1999: The 925 Split

As the East Bay's inland communities — Walnut Creek, Concord, Livermore, Pleasanton — continued to grow, the 510 number pool again came under pressure. In **1999**, the **925 area code** was split from 510, taking the inland portions of Contra Costa County and the Tri-Valley area.

After the 925 split, 510 settled into its current geographic footprint: the immediate East Bay cities closest to the bay itself.

### No Overlay (Yet)

Unlike many high-density urban area codes, 510 has not yet required an overlay area code. However, number exhaustion is monitored regularly by the California Public Utilities Commission. Businesses planning long-term operations in the East Bay should be aware that an overlay could eventually be introduced.

## Is the 510 Area Code Legitimate? Common Scam Awareness

Receiving a call from an unknown 510 number? Here's what you need to know.

**Legitimate 510 calls come from:**

- Oakland and East Bay residents and businesses
- University of California, Berkeley departments
- Bay Area healthcare providers
- Logistics and port-related businesses
- Local government agencies

**Warning signs of spam or scam calls:**

- Caller claims to be the IRS, Social Security Administration, or a utility company demanding immediate payment
- Robocall with no live agent when answered
- Caller refuses to provide a call-back number or business name
- Pressure tactics or threats

Like every area code, 510 numbers can be spoofed. Scammers use local area codes to increase answer rates — a known technique called **neighbor spoofing**. If something feels wrong, hang up and call the organization directly using a number from their official website.

**Legitimate businesses calling you from 510 will:**

- Identify themselves clearly
- Not demand payment via gift card or wire transfer
- Allow you to call back through published numbers
