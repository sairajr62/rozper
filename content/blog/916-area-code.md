## How to Read a 916 Area Code Number

Understanding how a 916 number is structured helps businesses and individuals dial correctly and ensure their calls connect every time.

![How to Read a 916 Area Code Number](/images/blog/916 image 1.webp)

### The Local Look: 10 Digits are Key

A complete 916 area code phone number follows the standard North American format: **(916) XXX-XXXX**. This 10-digit format has been mandatory in the Sacramento region since the introduction of the 279 overlay in 2018. Even local calls within the 916 zone must now be dialed with all 10 digits — simply dialing the 7-digit local number will not connect.

- **Area code:** 916
- **Exchange (prefix):** 3-digit central office code (e.g., 555)
- **Subscriber number:** 4-digit individual line number

### The Full Picture: Adding the Country Code

For international callers dialing into a 916 number, the full format is:

**+1 (916) XXX-XXXX**

The "+1" is the country code for the United States and Canada under the NANP. When configuring a VoIP system or CRM, always store 916 numbers in E.164 format (+19XXXXXXXXXX) to ensure compatibility with international dialing and automated communication platforms.

## The 916 Overlay: Technical Implications for Users and Businesses

In 2018, the California Public Utilities Commission introduced area code **279** as an overlay for the same geographic region as 916. This was necessary because the pool of available 916 numbers was nearing exhaustion due to population growth and the explosion of mobile devices and VoIP lines.

![The 916 Overlay: Technical Implications for Users and Businesses](/images/blog/916 image 2.webp)

### What is an Area Code Overlay? A Technical Explanation

An **overlay** is a second area code introduced on top of an existing one, covering the same geographic territory. Unlike a geographic split — where one region keeps the old code and another region gets the new one — an overlay means new subscribers in Sacramento may receive either a 916 or a 279 number. Both are equally local. Both reach the same geographic area.

The overlay approach avoids the disruption of forcing businesses and residents to change their existing numbers. However, it does require one immediate operational change: **mandatory 10-digit dialing**.

### Mandatory 10-Digit Dialing: A Non-Negotiable Protocol

With two area codes (916 and 279) sharing the same geographic region, 7-digit dialing became ambiguous and was discontinued. Since the overlay took effect, **all calls within the Sacramento region must be dialed with all 10 digits**, including the area code. This applies to:

- Local calls between 916 numbers
- Local calls between 279 numbers
- Calls between 916 and 279 numbers
- Speed dial and auto-dial systems programmed with 7-digit numbers

Businesses that had 7-digit entries in their CRM, PBX, or IVR systems needed to update them when the overlay launched. Any system still using 7-digit local dialing in Sacramento will fail to connect.

### Implications for Businesses: Ensuring Seamless Communication

For businesses with legacy phone systems, the overlay created a mandatory audit opportunity. ## Protect Your 916 Number: Easy Ways to Avoid Scam Calls

The same local trust that makes a 916 number valuable to legitimate businesses also makes it a tool of choice for phone scammers targeting Sacramento residents. Understanding the threat landscape helps you protect both your number and your customers.

### Common Phone Scams in the 916 Area Code Region

**Neighbor spoofing** is the most prevalent scam type affecting the Sacramento area. Fraudsters use software to display a 916 prefix on their outgoing calls, making the call appear to come from a local number — even if the actual caller is overseas. Recipients who might otherwise ignore a 1-800 number are more likely to answer what looks like a Sacramento call.

Common scam scripts targeting 916 numbers include:

- **IRS and tax authority impersonation** — especially prevalent during tax season, callers claim the recipient owes back taxes payable immediately.
- **Sacramento Utilities scam** — callers impersonate SMUD (Sacramento Municipal Utility District) or PG&E, threatening service shutoff unless payment is made immediately.
- **Tech support fraud** — callers claim to be from Microsoft or Apple, warning of a virus on the recipient's device.
- **Lottery and sweepstakes fraud** — recipients are told they've won a prize but must pay fees to claim it.

### Protection Strategies for Your 916 Area Code Number

**For businesses:**
- Register with the **FCC's Do-Not-Originate list** to prevent your number from being spoofed.
- Enable **STIR/SHAKEN call authentication** on your VoIP platform — Rozper supports this standard, which verifies caller identity before delivery.
- Use **call analytics** to monitor unusual outbound call patterns that might indicate your number has been spoofed or compromised.
- Inform customers through your website or onboarding materials about what legitimate calls from your 916 number will and won't ask for.

**For individuals:**
- If you receive an unexpected 916 call requesting payment or personal information, hang up and call the organization directly using a number from their official website.
- Register with the **FTC's National Do Not Call Registry** at donotcall.gov.
- Use carrier-provided spam filtering or third-party apps (Nomorobo, Hiya) to flag suspected scam calls.
