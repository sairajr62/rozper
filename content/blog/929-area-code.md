## What Is the 929 Area Code?

![Map highlighting the outer boroughs of New York City served by the 929 prefix](/images/blog/what-is-the-929-area-code.webp)

The **929 area code** is a NANP telephone code assigned to New York City. It launched on April 16, 2011. It was added as an overlay to the existing 718 and 347 codes. Those older codes were running out of number combinations. The city's population and business growth used them up fast.

The **929 area code** is part of the 718/347/917/929 overlay complex. That makes it a unique piece of US phone history. This was the first region in the country served by more than three area codes at once. It shows just how dense and fast New York City grows.

The **929 area code** runs on Eastern Time. That is UTC−5 in standard time and UTC−4 during Daylight Saving. Every caller using **area code 929** must dial all 10 digits for local calls. This rule applies across every NYC overlay code.

## A Brief History of the 929 Area Code

![Timeline illustration of the 2011 overlay that introduced the 929 area code to New York City](/images/blog/a-brief-history-of-the-929-area-code.webp)

New York City has always pushed its phone numbering system to the limit. By the early 2000s, the original 718 code was running low. It had served the outer boroughs for decades. The 347 overlay was added to help. But mobile phones and connected devices used up 347 fast too.

So in 2011, the New York Public Service Commission approved a new code: 929. Regulators chose another overlay instead of a geographic split. That meant no one had to change their existing number. New customers in Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island simply received numbers from the new 929 pool.

Today, four codes share the same territory: 718, 347, 917, and 929. That makes New York City one of the most complex area code regions in North America.

**What is the difference between the 929, 718, and 347 area codes?**
