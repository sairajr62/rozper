## What Is the 210 Area Code?

![San Antonio Texas 210 Coverage Map](/images/blog/210-image-1.webp)

The **210 area code** is a NANPA-assigned telephone code covering San Antonio and the surrounding south-central Texas region. It was established in 1992 as a geographic split from the 512 area code, which had covered all of central Texas since the original NANPA assignment in 1947. San Antonio's steady population growth eventually required additional number capacity, and the 726 area code was introduced as an overlay — meaning both 210 and 726 now serve the same geographic area without geographic distinction.

## The History of San Antonio's 210: From 512 to Its Own Identity

![San Antonio 210 Phone Code History](/images/blog/210-image-2.webp)

When NANPA established the original US area codes in 1947, all of central and south Texas — Austin, San Antonio, Corpus Christi — shared the [512 area code](/blog/512-area-code). For decades, that single code held. The mobile phone explosion of the late 1980s and early 1990s changed the calculus everywhere.

**1992:** The Texas Public Utility Commission approved a geographic split, assigning the new 210 to San Antonio and south-central Texas. Austin and surrounding central Texas retained 512. The split gave San Antonio its own telephonic identity for the first time.

**Later years:** As San Antonio's population grew into one of the fastest-expanding metros in the country — adding hundreds of thousands of new residents per decade — even 210 began approaching number exhaustion. Rather than a second geographic split, regulators introduced the 726 area code as an overlay, preserving the existing 210 numbers while making new inventory available under both codes. Much like how the [713 area code](/blog/713-area-code) serves Houston alongside its overlay codes, both 210 and 726 are now valid San Antonio area codes with no geographic boundary between them.

The **210 area code** carries more than 30 years of San Antonio identity — making it the code businesses reach for when local recognition matters most. No other San Antonio prefix holds that history.

## Understanding the 210 and 726 Overlay

The 726 area code was introduced as a full overlay for the same south-central Texas region as 210. Here is what it means in practice:

**Same geography.** Both 210 and 726 serve every address in the same counties. There is no city or neighborhood that belongs exclusively to one code.

**New numbers, either code.** When a new line is provisioned in San Antonio today, the carrier may assign either a 210 or a 726 number. Virtual phone providers often carry inventory from both codes.

**Mandatory 10-digit dialing.** Because 210 and 726 are a full overlay, you must always include the area code — 10-digit dialing is required for all calls within the region. Dialing only 7 digits will not connect.

**Recognition gap.** Despite identical coverage, 210 carries 30+ years of San Antonio brand recognition. Established businesses, military contractors, healthcare institutions, and longstanding local firms overwhelmingly hold 210 numbers. A 726 number is functionally identical but reads as newer.

## Time Zone and Dialing Rules

The **210 area code** operates in **Central Time (CT) — CST (UTC−6) in winter, CDT (UTC−5) in summer**.

San Antonio observes Daylight Saving Time, so the clock shifts twice per year — one hour forward in mid-March, one hour back in early November.

**Time comparison vs. other major zones:**

| Time Zone | Offset vs. 210 (CT) |
|---|---|
| Eastern (ET) | +1 hour |
| Mountain (MT) | −1 hour |
| Pacific (PT) | −2 hours |
| UTC | +6 hours (CST) / +5 hours (CDT) |

**Dialing format:**
- Domestic: 1 + 210 (or 726) + 7-digit number
- International: your country's exit code + 1 + 210 + 7-digit number

10-digit dialing is standard throughout the San Antonio metro. If your phone system stores any San Antonio number without the full **area code 210** prefix, update it — 7-digit dialing will not connect.

## Is the 210 Area Code a Scam?

The **210 area code** is legitimate — officially assigned by NANPA to San Antonio and south-central Texas. Like every major US metro code, it sees spoofed scam calls. Scammers target 210 specifically because San Antonio residents are more likely to answer what appears to be a local call.

**Common 210 scam patterns in 2026:**

- **Insurance impersonation (the dominant 210 scam)** — Health insurance calls account for 11.4% of all 210 spam complaints. Callers claim to represent carriers like Allstate, Farmers, Liberty Mutual, or State Farm and offer discounted coverage or policy reviews. These calls originate from overseas call centers using spoofed 210 numbers to appear local.
- **Medicare/Medicaid fraud** — Second-most-reported at 9.07% of complaints. Callers target older San Antonio residents — a significant demographic given JBSA's large veteran and retiree population — with fake Medicare enrollment offers, supplement upsells, and benefit verification requests.
- **Auto warranty robocalls** — Pre-recorded messages warn of expiring vehicle warranties and prompt the recipient to "press 1" to speak with a representative who will capture personal and financial information.

**How to protect yourself:**
1. Let unfamiliar 210 numbers go to voicemail before calling back
2. Never share insurance policy numbers, Medicare IDs, or financial information with unsolicited callers
3. Verify any insurance offer by calling the carrier directly using the number on your card
4. Report scam calls to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov or the FCC consumer complaint portal

STIR/SHAKEN caller authentication — required by the FCC for US carriers — increasingly marks spoofed 210 numbers as "Scam Likely" on compliant networks. Legitimate business numbers from verified VoIP providers pass authentication and display cleanly on caller ID.
