## The History of San Antonio's 210: From 512 to Its Own Identity

When the first US area codes launched in 1947, all of central and south Texas shared the 512 code. Then came the mobile phone boom of the late 1980s. That growth changed everything.

**1992:** The Texas Public Utility Commission approved a split by area. It gave 210 to San Antonio and south-central Texas. Austin kept 512. This gave San Antonio its own phone identity. Regulators later added the 726 overlay. It helped handle new number demand without another split. Today, the **210 area code** carries over 30 years of San Antonio identity. It's the code businesses reach for when local trust matters most. Just as [the 281 area code](/blog/281-area-code) shaped Houston's identity, 210 anchors south-central Texas.

## Understanding the 210 and 726 Overlay

The 726 area code is a full overlay for the same south-central Texas region. Both codes serve every address in the same counties. You must dial 10 digits for every call. The coverage is the same, but area code 210 still carries over 30 years of San Antonio brand trust. That's a clear edge for businesses that want a strong local name.

## Time Zone and Dialing Rules

The **210 area code** runs on **Central Time — CST (UTC−6) in winter, CDT (UTC−5) in summer**. San Antonio observes Daylight Saving Time. That puts it one hour behind Eastern and one hour ahead of Mountain. To dial within the US, use 1 + 210 + the 7-digit number. Ten-digit dialing is required. A 7-digit dial will not connect.

## Is the 210 Area Code a Scam?

![How Caller ID Spoofing Works and How to Protect Yourself](/images/blog/210-image-4.webp)

The **210 area code** is real and safe to use. It covers San Antonio and south-central Texas. The [City of San Antonio](https://www.sanantonio.gov/) keeps official facts and community news for the area. Like every major metro code, it does see fake scam calls. Locals are simply more likely to pick up a familiar number, so scammers aim for it.

**Common 210 scam patterns in 2026:** Fake insurance calls make up 11.4% of all 210 spam reports. Fake Medicare and Medicaid calls rank second at 9.07%. These target veterans and older residents. Auto warranty robocalls also push people to "press 1." That trick is used to steal financial data.

You can protect yourself in a few simple ways. Send unknown 210 numbers to voicemail. Never share Medicare IDs with callers you did not contact first. Report fraud to the FTC. A system called STIR/SHAKEN marks spoofed numbers as "Scam Likely." Real VoIP business numbers, by contrast, show up cleanly on caller ID.
