# The 877 Area Code: A Clear Guide to This Toll-Free Prefix

A phone number can say a lot before anyone even answers. The 877 area code tells a caller one simple thing: this call is free. That small signal builds trust fast. It also gives a business a national feel, no matter where the team sits. This guide covers what the prefix is, how a call routes, what it costs, and how to get one set up.

## What the 877 Area Code Is

![Illustration of a caller reaching a business for free through a toll-free phone line](/images/blog/877-what-is-the-877-area-code.webp)

The 877 area code is a toll-free prefix. It is used across the North American Numbering Plan, which covers the United States, Canada, and nearby territories. It is not tied to any single city or state. A local prefix works that way, but a toll-free prefix does not. When a caller dials a number on this prefix, the business that owns the line pays for the call. The caller pays nothing at all. That one rule is the whole point of toll-free service. Customers can reach a company from anywhere without worrying about the cost. The business, in turn, gets a number that speaks for the whole country.

## The History Behind the 877 Prefix

The 877 area code was introduced in 1998. It was the second toll-free prefix ever added to the North American Numbering Plan. The very first toll-free prefix was 800, launched by AT&T in 1966. As 800 numbers ran low, the industry added the [888 area code](https://www.rozper.com/blog/888-area-code/) in 1996. Two years later, 877 followed to keep up with growing demand. More prefixes came after that. 866 arrived in 2000, then 855 in 2010, 844 in 2013, and 833 in 2017. Every one of these prefixes, including 877, works in exactly the same way. They exist only to open up fresh number combinations once older pools ran dry.

## How an 877 Call Connects

![Diagram showing an 877 call routed from a caller through a switch to a desk phone or cloud app](/images/blog/877-how-does-the-877-area-code-work.webp)

An 877 number uses the standard ten-digit format. That means the prefix plus a seven-digit line number. There are no special codes to dial. A caller just enters all ten digits from anywhere in North America. Behind the scenes, the number is managed in one place. It gets routed to whatever spot the business chooses. That could be a desk phone, a mobile device, a call center, or a cloud app. The routing can change at any time. Calls can be split by time of day or by team. Every inbound call can be logged, too. In practice, an 877 number acts less like a single phone line. It works more like a flexible front door into the business.

## What an 877 Line Costs

Toll-free pricing tends to be low and easy to predict. Most providers charge a small monthly fee for the number itself. They also add a per-minute rate for inbound calls. The business pays this cost, which is why the caller pays nothing. Vanity numbers that spell a word often carry a one-time premium. Demand for those combinations runs high. Plain numeric lines cost less and work just as well for most companies. Many plans bundle in call routing, voicemail, and analytics at no extra charge. So the real cost tracks call volume more than setup. There is no equipment to buy and no technician visit needed. A brand-new business can launch a national-sounding phone line in a single afternoon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the 877 area code free to call?**
Yes. Calls to an 877 area code number are free for the person dialing. That holds true from anywhere in the United States, Canada, and other North American Numbering Plan regions. The business that owns the line covers the full cost of the call.

**Where is the 877 area code located?**
Nowhere in particular. Area code 877 is a toll-free prefix, not a place marker. A number using it can be answered in any state or province. A shop in Ohio and a firm in Toronto could both run an 877 line from the same dashboard.

**When was the 877 area code introduced?**
The 877 area code launched in 1998. It was the second toll-free prefix added after the original 800 pool ran low, following 888, which arrived in 1996.

**How is an 877 number different from an 800 number?**
There is no real difference in how they work. Both are toll-free and free to the caller. Only the digits differ. When the 800 pool filled up, prefixes like 888 and 877 were added to expand supply, and each one carries the same benefits as a classic 800 line.

**Can I port my existing toll-free number to a new provider?**
Usually, yes. Most providers let you move a current toll-free number, including one on the 877 area code, to a new platform. You supply authorization and account details, and the transfer often finishes with no downtime.
