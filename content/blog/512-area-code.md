# 512 Area Code: Austin, Texas Complete Business Number Guide

Few prefixes carry as much regional weight as the 512 area code. It has been tied to the Texas capital since the first phone numbering plans went live. That history still shapes how Austin-area customers decide whether to trust a new call. This guide covers where the code reaches. It shows the decades of splits behind it. It also shows how to get a 512 number for your own team.

## What Is the 512 Area Code?

The 512 area code was set up in **October 1947**. It was one of the first area codes made under the North American Numbering Plan. At the time, it covered a huge stretch of south Texas. That range ran from the Gulf Coast down to the Mexican border.

| Time Zone | Central Time (CT) — UTC−6/UTC−5 |

That footprint shrank twice as demand grew. This differs from smaller, single-city codes. [The 210 area code](/blog/210-area-code) in nearby San Antonio is one example. It split away from this same area back in 1992.

## The History Behind Area Code 512

![Timeline of the 512 area code's splits and overlay](/images/blog/512-the-history-behind-area-code-512.webp)

This code dates back to October 1947. It was one of the first area codes made when the North American Numbering Plan first launched. At the time it covered a huge stretch of south Texas. That included San Antonio, Corpus Christi, and the Rio Grande Valley.

That footprint shrank twice as demand grew. In 1992, San Antonio and the Rio Grande Valley split off into area code 210. Then in 1999, the coastal area around Corpus Christi split off again into 361. That left this code focused on Austin and the nearby hill country. By 2013, growth had caught up once more. Regulators added 737 on top of the same area as an overlay. They did this instead of splitting the region a third time. Every existing 512 number kept working unchanged. Ten-digit dialing became a must as a result. Two Austin firms a few blocks apart might now use different three-digit codes. Yet both still dial each other the same way.

## 512 Area Code Time Zone

Austin and the rest of the 512 area sit in the **Central Time Zone**. The region runs on **CST** (UTC−6) from early November through mid-March. It runs on **CDT** (UTC−5) the rest of the year. Much of the tech and university work in Austin follows CT hours. Because of this, teams calling from the East Coast should plan outreach before 5 PM ET. That gives them the best shot at reaching Austin offices before the day ends. West Coast callers have an easier window. CT sits only two hours ahead of PT.

## Is a 512 Area Code Call a Scam?

Because area code 512 is so well known locally, scammers spoof it a lot. Reported scams include fake Social Security threats and bogus "you bought an iPhone" alerts. Others fake calls from the Texas Comptroller or the City of Austin. They ask for payment through gift cards or wire transfers. The [FTC's National Do Not Call Registry FAQ](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/national-do-not-call-registry-faqs) explains how real firms should handle outbound calls. This makes it easier to spot the fake ones.

None of this makes the 512 area code itself unsafe. It just means a call demanding quick payment deserves a second look. Hang up. Look up the group on your own. Call back using a number you find yourself, not one the caller gives you. Firms running a real 512 line should use a STIR/SHAKEN-compliant VoIP provider. This way, recipients see a verified caller ID instead of a flagged number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What cities does the 512 area code cover today?**
It covers Austin and the surrounding Central Texas suburbs, including Round Rock, Cedar Park, Georgetown, San Marcos, and Kyle, after two geographic splits reduced its original 1947 footprint.

**When was area code 512 created?**
It was one of the original area codes assigned in October 1947, back when it covered most of south-central Texas, including San Antonio and Corpus Christi.

**Is 737 the same as area code 512?**
No. Area code 737 is an overlay added in 2013 that shares the same Austin-area territory rather than replacing it. Existing 512 numbers kept working, but the overlay made ten-digit dialing mandatory.

**Can a business outside Texas get a 512 area code number?**
Yes. Virtual number providers can issue one to any business and forward calls to whatever phone or system the team already uses, without requiring a physical office in Austin.

**Are all calls from this prefix legitimate?**
Not automatically. Scammers spoof local numbers, including this one, to appear trustworthy. Verify unexpected calls about payments, benefits, or legal threats independently before responding.

**How long does it take to activate a 512 number?**
Most VoIP providers turn on a new 512 number within minutes of purchase. Porting an old number from another carrier can take a few business days instead.
