## History of the 559 Area Code

![California Number Plan Pyramid Diagram Under the NANP](/images/blog/559-image-3.webp)

### California's 209: The Original Central Valley Code

Before area code 559 existed, Central California used **area code 209**. This code had served the San Joaquin Valley since the North American Numbering Plan started in 1947. The mobile phone boom of the 1990s changed that. Pager and fax lines also grew fast. Together, they pushed 209 toward exhaustion.

### November 14, 1998: 559 Created

On **November 14, 1998**, area code 559 was created. It served Fresno, Madera, Kings, and Tulare counties after the CNCA flagged 209 as nearly full. The northern portion — Stockton and Modesto — kept the 209 prefix.

### March 26, 2025: The 357 Overlay Launches

By the 2020s, growth in mobile devices, VoIP, and IoT pushed the 559 number supply toward exhaustion again. The CPUC approved the **357 area code** as an overlay. This answered the steady growth the [City of Fresno](https://www.fresno.gov/) has driven as the commercial anchor of California's Central Valley. The new code went live on **March 26, 2025**. **Mandatory 10-digit dialing** took effect in Q1 2025.

## 559 Area Code Time Zone

The **559 area code** sits in the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**. It runs PST (UTC−8) from early November to mid-March. It runs PDT (UTC−7) from mid-March to early November.

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**East Coast (ET):** 3 hours ahead. Call by 9 AM PT. **Central Time (CT):** 2 hours ahead, with solid overlap. **Mountain Time (MT):** 1 hour ahead. This is the easiest pair for scheduling calls.

## Is a 559 Area Code Call a Scam?

The **559 area code** is real. But like all US area codes, scammers can fake its caller ID. Several scam types show up in the region.

**Fake Amazon Purchase Alerts:** Robocalls claim someone made a big purchase on the recipient's account. Amazon does not make cold calls about specific purchases.

**PG&E Utility Shutoff Threats:** Scammers pose as PG&E workers. They threaten a shutoff right away unless the person pays fast. Real PG&E cutoffs come with written notices first.

**Medicare "Final Notice" Scams:** Callers claim Medicare coverage is about to end. Real Medicare staff do not make cold calls that warn of a sudden benefit cutoff.

**Social Security Administration Impersonation:** Fake voicemails claim odd SSN activity, with legal action on the way. The SSA does not leave voicemails that threaten you.

**Law Enforcement Impersonation:** Scammers have posed as the **Fresno County Sheriff's Office**. They claim there is a warrant and ask for payment. The Sheriff's Office never asks for money by phone.

Never pay a fine or share account details after a cold call you did not expect.
