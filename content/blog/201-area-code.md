## What Is the 201 Area Code?

The **201 area code** is one of the most historically significant telephone codes in the United States. Established in 1947 as the original statewide New Jersey area code under the North American Numbering Plan (NANP), it now serves only Hudson County and the majority of Bergen County.

## History of the 201 Area Code

![New Jersey 201 Prefix History Timeline 1947 to 2001](/images/blog/201-image-4.webp)

The **201 area code** was the very first area code assigned to New Jersey, serving the entire state when the NANP launched in **1947**. Population growth led to splits that progressively reduced its territory.

### The 1991 Split: 908 Created

On **June 8, 1991**, the southern and central regions were carved out to form the **908 area code**, leaving 201 to cover northern New Jersey.

### The 1997 Split: 973 Created

On **June 1, 1997**, Essex County and Passaic, Morris, and Sussex counties were reassigned to the new **973 area code**, reducing 201 to its current Hudson and Bergen County footprint.

### 2001: The 551 Overlay

Mobile proliferation and new business lines exhausted the 201 number pool, leading to the **551 area code** on **December 1, 2001** — New Jersey's first overlay — covering the identical territory as 201 and making **10-digit dialing** mandatory. Additional resources on telecommunications and business regulations in the region are available through the [New Jersey state government](https://www.nj.gov/).

## 201 Area Code Time Zone

![10-Digit Dialing and Eastern Time Zone Guide](/images/blog/201-image-2.webp)

This region operates in the **Eastern Time Zone (ET)** — the same as New York City:

- **Eastern Standard Time (EST):** UTC−5 — early November to mid-March
- **Eastern Daylight Time (EDT):** UTC−4 — mid-March to early November

Because area code 201 shares Eastern Time with Manhattan, cross-Hudson businesses face no time zone adjustment.

## Is a 201 Area Code Call a Scam?

The **201 area code** prefix is entirely legitimate, but its high regional recognition makes it a target for **caller ID spoofing**.

### OSHA Compliance Scams

Callers leave urgent voicemails claiming OSHA non-compliance, then attempt to sell unnecessary safety training materials. Real OSHA enforcement arrives in writing, not by unsolicited phone call.

### Utility Company Switching Fraud

Callers impersonate PSE&G or Elizabethtown Gas, collect an account number during a fake "supply charge review," and switch the customer to a third-party supplier with higher rates without consent.

### Neighbor Spoofing

Scammers display a 201 number that closely matches your own prefix to increase answer rates. Use a reverse phone lookup before returning suspicious calls, and choose a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider to reduce spoofing risk.
