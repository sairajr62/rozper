## Where Is the 516?

The **516 area code** covers Nassau County. This is the westernmost county on Long Island. It sits just east of Queens. The code began in 1951. That year, the old 212 area code split to handle new demand. A second split came in 1999. It created area code 631 for Suffolk County. After that, 516 served only Nassau County.

![Where Is the 516?](/images/blog/516-image-1.webp)

Growth continued. In 2017, the region added the 363 overlay. New phone lines in the area may now get 363 numbers. Old **516 area code** numbers keep their same prefix. Everyone in the region must dial all ten digits for local calls.

For a business, a 516 number sends a clear signal. It says you belong to Nassau County. That's different from the wider Long Island 631 market. It's also separate from the [917 area code](/blog/917-area-code) area.

## Protect Your 516 Number: Easy Ways to Avoid Scam Calls

Nassau County has high incomes. That makes **516 area code** numbers a target for scammers. Fraudsters go after wealthy suburban codes. Why? Residents there handle more financial deals, professional services, and real estate. These are all high-value targets.

### Common Phone Scams in the 516 Area Code Region

**Neighbor spoofing:** Scammers use 516 prefixes to copy local numbers. They bet that residents will answer a call that looks familiar. Then the script starts. It might be a fake bank alert, an IRS demand, or a utility shutoff threat.

**Real estate and mortgage scams:** Nassau County's housing market stays busy. Scammers pose as mortgage lenders, title companies, or attorneys. They ask for wire transfers or financial data. Often, the call comes from a spoofed 516 number.

**Healthcare and Medicare fraud:** Nassau County has many older residents. Scammers pose as Medicare staff or providers. They ask for personal insurance and billing details.

**Business impostor scams:** Callers claim to represent county offices, chambers, or registries. Then they ask for fees. These cover listings, permits, or filings that don't really exist.

### How to Protect Yourself

- **Verify before acting:** Hang up. Then call the organization back using a number from its official website. Never use the number that called you.
- **Register on the Do Not Call Registry:** Sign up for free at the [national Do Not Call registry](https://www.donotcall.gov/). This cuts down on unwanted sales calls a lot.
- **Enable carrier-level call screening:** Most major carriers offer spam filtering. Turn it on in your account settings.
- **Never wire money by phone:** Real banks, agencies, and title companies don't ask for wire transfers on an unsolicited call.
- **Report suspicious numbers:** File a complaint with the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov. You can also report to the FCC at consumercomplaints.fcc.gov.

Legitimate businesses share the job of building caller trust. Keep a consistent name, a listed number, and steady call patterns. That kind of professional presence shows that your 516 area code number is real.
