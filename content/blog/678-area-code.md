## What Is the 678 and Where Does It Apply?

The 678 area code serves the Atlanta, Georgia metropolitan area and a broad stretch of the surrounding metro region. It was put into service on January 6, 1998 as an overlay to the existing 404 and 770 area codes. Area code 678 responded to the rapid exhaustion of available numbers during Atlanta's late-1990s economic and technology boom.

Unlike a geographic split, an overlay means 678 operates across the same territory as its companions — all codes coexist in the same city blocks and counties. When 678 launched, ten-digit dialing became mandatory across the entire metro area. Atlanta's continued growth added area code 470 in 2010 and area code 943 in March 2022, bringing the current stack to four: 404, 678, 770, and 470/943. The 678 area code is in the **Eastern Time Zone**, observing Daylight Saving Time from March through November.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What cities are covered by the 678 area code?**
The 678 area code covers 50 cities across the greater Atlanta metro region. Key locations include Atlanta, Sandy Springs, Alpharetta, Roswell, Johns Creek, Marietta, Smyrna, Dunwoody, Gainesville, and Cartersville. It spans 27 counties and 92 zip codes, covering the full breadth of Georgia's primary commercial zone.

**What is the difference between area codes 678, 404, 470, and 770?**
All four area codes serve the same general Atlanta metropolitan region as overlays, meaning they cover the same geographic territory. The 404 code is Atlanta's original area code, 770 was added in 1995 for suburban growth, 678 followed in 1998, and 470 and 943 were introduced in 2010 and 2022 respectively to meet ongoing demand. In practice, all four codes are geographically interchangeable within the metro.

**Does Georgia require both parties to consent to call recording?**
No. Georgia is a one-party consent state. Under Georgia law, only one party to a phone call needs to be aware of and consent to the recording — typically you or a member of your team. This is distinct from two-party consent states like California, where all parties must be informed. Businesses should still consider applicable federal regulations and any industry-specific compliance requirements relevant to their sector.
