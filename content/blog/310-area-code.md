## History of the 310 Area Code

The **310 area code** was created on **November 2, 1991**, when the original 213 area code — which had covered essentially all of Southern California since 1947 — could no longer meet demand. The western and southern portions of LA County were carved out as the new 310 territory.

Area code 310 was the first major split from the 213 territory, immediately establishing itself as the identifier for LA's most commercially influential western corridor.

**Key milestones:**

**1991 — Creation:** 310 split from 213, taking the Westside, South Bay, and coastal communities. This was the first major separation of the LA Westside from the [213 area code](/blog/213-area-code), which retained Downtown Los Angeles and the central city as its core territory.

**1997 — The 562 Split:** On January 25, 1997, the 310 territory was further divided. The southeast portion of Los Angeles County — Long Beach, Downey, and surrounding communities — was reassigned to the new **562 area code**, shrinking the area code 310 footprint to its current boundaries.

**2006 — The 424 Overlay:** On July 26, 2006, the [424 area code](/blog/424-area-code) was added as an overlay to the 310 region. This was the first overlay implemented in California. Unlike a geographic split, the overlay did not move any existing numbers — it simply added a new pool of numbers for the same geography. This made 310 numbers scarcer, which paradoxically increased their perceived value.

**Mandatory 10-digit dialing:** The 424 overlay introduced mandatory 10-digit dialing for all local calls within the 310/424 region. Simply dialing a 7-digit number no longer works — the full area code must be included.

The overlay sparked notable community pushback. The politically active Westside community objected to losing the simplicity of 7-digit local dialing — a testament to how deeply this prefix had embedded itself in the regional identity.

## 310 Area Code Time Zone

![Pacific Time Zone Best Calling Hours Infographic](/images/blog/310-image-4.webp)

This region operates entirely within the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**:

- **Pacific Standard Time (PST):** UTC−8 — observed from early November to mid-March
- **Pacific Daylight Time (PDT):** UTC−7 — observed from mid-March to early November

### Practical Calling Considerations

**From the East Coast:** Eastern Time (ET) is 3 hours ahead of Pacific Time. A 9 AM PT call reaches New York at noon ET — ideal for cross-coast business coordination.

**From the Midwest (Central Time):** CT is 2 hours ahead. An 11 AM CT call reaches the 310 zone at 9 AM PT.

**From Mountain Time:** MT is 1 hour ahead of PT. Note that Arizona (except Navajo Nation) does not observe Daylight Saving Time, which occasionally creates a 2-hour difference during DST transitions.

**International calls to 310:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 310 + 7-digit number. From Australia, dial 0011 + 1 + 310 + 7-digit number. On mobile phones globally, use +1-310-XXX-XXXX.

**Best window for business calls to 310:** 9 AM – 5 PM PT keeps you within standard business hours. Avoid calling before 9 AM PT, as the 3-hour East Coast advantage makes it tempting to start too early.

## Is a 310 Area Code Call a Scam?

The 310 prefix is completely legitimate — it serves millions of real residents and businesses across the LA Westside and South Bay. However, its high recognition and strong association with affluent, trusted communities makes it a prime target for caller ID spoofing.

Scammers frequently spoof 310 numbers because California residents — and people nationwide — are more likely to answer a call displaying a familiar, prestigious local code.

### Common Scam Types to Watch For

**Fake IRS or government calls:** Callers impersonate IRS agents or government officials and claim the recipient owes back taxes, faces an arrest warrant, or is under investigation. Legitimate government agencies do not initiate contact this way.

**Tech support fraud:** Callers claim to be from Apple, Microsoft, or Google support, stating that the recipient's device has been compromised and requesting remote access or payment. No legitimate tech company cold-calls customers about device issues.

**Prize and lottery scams:** Callers claim the recipient has won a prize tied to an LA-based promotion and request a processing fee or personal details to claim it.

**Medical and prescription spam:** The 310 zone has seen elevated reports of calls impersonating healthcare providers or insurance companies requesting personal health or payment information.

### How to Stay Protected

- Never provide personal information, payment details, or remote device access in response to an unsolicited call.
- If the caller demands gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency — hang up immediately. No legitimate business or government agency operates this way.
- Use a free reverse phone lookup to check unfamiliar numbers before returning calls.
- Choose a VoIP provider that supports **STIR/SHAKEN** caller authentication — this framework verifies caller ID data and reduces spoofing effectiveness.

For businesses with legitimate 310 numbers, proactively communicating your contact policies to customers helps protect your brand during periods of elevated spoofing activity.
