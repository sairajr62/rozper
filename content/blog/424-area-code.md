## What Is the 424 Area Code?

![LA Westside 424 Code Overview](/images/blog/424-image-1.webp)

The **424 area code** is a California telephone area code serving the western and southwestern portions of Los Angeles County. It operates as an **overlay** of the 310 area code, meaning both codes serve the exact same geographic region and both are equally local to residents and businesses in that territory.

**Quick Facts:**

| Detail | Info |

| Area Code | 424 |
| Relationship | Overlay of 310 area code |
| State | California |
| Region | LA Westside, South Bay, Beach Cities |
| Time Zone | Pacific Time (PT) — UTC−8/UTC−7 |
| Dialing | 10-digit mandatory |
| Officially Launched | July 26, 2006 |

Area code 424 is California's first overlay code — introduced when the existing 310 number pool could no longer keep up with demand from the region's booming mobile, VoIP, and business growth.

Because 424 overlays 310 — rather than replacing it — every existing 310 number remained intact when 424 launched. New subscribers in the region may receive either a 310 or a 424 number. Both are recognized as local LA numbers.

## History of the 424 Area Code

The **424 area code** was officially launched on **July 26, 2006**, but its story begins decades earlier with the original 213 area code that once served all of Southern California.

### From 213 to 310 to 424

In 1947, the entire Los Angeles region operated under the 213 area code. By **November 2, 1991**, demand had grown enough to carve out the [310 area code](/blog/310-area-code) for the Westside and South Bay. Then in **January 1997**, the southeast portion of the 310 territory split off to become the [562 area code](/blog/562-area-code), leaving 310 concentrated in its current footprint.

By the late 1990s, it was already clear that 310 numbers were being exhausted by the surge in mobile devices and business growth.

### The 1999 Protest and the Consumer Area Code Relief Act

What makes the 424 history unusual is the organized public resistance it encountered before it even launched. In **1999**, Californians — particularly on the politically vocal LA Westside — pushed back hard against the proposed overlay. The opposition was strong enough that the **California State Assembly passed the Consumer Area Code Relief Act of 1999**, which temporarily tabled the overlay.

The primary objection: mandatory 10-digit dialing for local calls. The 310 community had grown accustomed to 7-digit local dialing, and the prospect of adding the area code to every call — even within the same neighborhood — was seen as an unnecessary disruption.

The relief lasted only a few years. By 2006, number exhaustion could no longer be avoided, and the overlay was implemented as planned.

### 2006 to Present

Once area code 424 launched in 2006, the transition went smoothly. Existing 310 subscribers retained their numbers entirely. New subscribers entering the market received 424 numbers from the fresh pool. Both codes now coexist as fully equivalent local identifiers for the same geography.

The overlay model has proven effective: the 310/424 dual-code system has supported the region's continued growth in mobile users, IoT devices, VoIP services, and business line proliferation without requiring any further geographic splits.

## 424 Area Code Time Zone

This region operates entirely within the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**:

- **Pacific Standard Time (PST):** UTC−8 — observed from early November to mid-March
- **Pacific Daylight Time (PDT):** UTC−7 — observed from mid-March to early November

### Calling Into the 424 Zone

**From the East Coast (ET):** Eastern Time is 3 hours ahead. A call at 9 AM PT reaches New York at noon ET — good for mid-morning cross-coast coordination.

**From the Midwest (CT):** Central Time is 2 hours ahead of Pacific. An 11 AM CT call hits the 424 zone at 9 AM PT — a solid morning-to-morning window.

**From Mountain Time (MT):** One hour ahead in most cases. Note that Arizona (excluding Navajo Nation) does not observe Daylight Saving Time, which creates a 2-hour gap during DST periods.

**International calls to 424:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 424 + 7-digit number. From Australia, dial 0011 + 1 + 424 + 7-digit number. Globally, use +1-424-XXX-XXXX format on mobile.

**Best calling window:** 9 AM to 5 PM PT keeps both parties in standard business hours. For businesses managing inbound call flows, ensuring your IVR business hours are set to Pacific Time prevents after-hours calls from going unanswered or landing incorrectly.

## Is a 424 Area Code Call a Scam?

The **424 area code** is legitimate — it serves real residents and businesses across LA's Westside and South Bay. However, its high name recognition and strong association with affluent communities make it a frequent target for **caller ID spoofing**, where scammers fake a local area code to increase the likelihood of an answer.

Several specific scam patterns have been reported targeting and impersonating 424 numbers.

### Common 424-Spoofed Scams

**Student loan forgiveness robocalls:** This is one of the most reported scam patterns specifically targeting California residents. Robocalls claim the recipient's loan forgiveness status is changing and request personal information or payment to process relief. The Department of Education does not initiate contact this way.

**Fake bank fraud alerts:** Callers impersonating major Los Angeles-area banks — including Chase, Wells Fargo, and Bank of America — claim unauthorized transactions have occurred and ask recipients to verify account credentials or transfer funds to a safe account. Legitimate banks never ask customers to transfer money or share passwords over an unsolicited call.

**Utility payment fraud:** Callers impersonating LADWP (Los Angeles Department of Water and Power) or SoCalGas claim an account is overdue and that service will be cut off unless immediate payment is made via prepaid card or wire transfer.

**Delivery phishing:** Callers or texts spoofing 424 numbers claim to be UPS, FedEx, or USPS with a package delivery issue requiring personal verification.

### Protecting Yourself and Your Business

- Legitimate government agencies, banks, and utilities do not demand immediate payment via gift card, cryptocurrency, or wire transfer over an unsolicited call.
- If a local number calls claiming urgency on any financial matter, hang up and call the organization directly using the official number on their website.
- Use a reverse phone lookup to check unfamiliar numbers before returning calls.
- Businesses operating legitimate 424 numbers benefit from using a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider — this authentication framework verifies caller ID data and significantly reduces spoofing effectiveness.
