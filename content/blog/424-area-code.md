## What Is the 424 Area Code?

The **424 area code** is a California phone area code. It serves the western and southwestern parts of Los Angeles County. It works as an **overlay** of the 310 area code. That means both codes cover the same area. Both are equally local for the people and businesses who live and work there.

## History of the 424 Area Code

The **424 area code** launched on **July 26, 2006**. It traces back to the original 213 code, which once served all of Southern California. By 1991, demand led to the new 310 area code for the Westside and South Bay. In 1997, the southeast part split off as 562. The [Federal Communications Commission](https://www.fcc.gov/) steps in to coordinate area code overlays whenever numbers start running out.

### The 1999 Protest and the Consumer Area Code Relief Act

424's history has an unusual twist: organized public pushback. In **1999**, LA Westside residents fought the proposed overlay. Their pressure led the **California State Assembly to pass the Consumer Area Code Relief Act of 1999**. This delayed the overlay for a while. By 2006, though, the region ran out of numbers, and the overlay became unavoidable. Existing 310 subscribers kept their numbers. New subscribers began receiving 424 numbers instead.

## 424 Area Code Time Zone

This whole region sits in the **Pacific Time Zone (PT)**. That means Pacific Standard Time (PST, UTC-8) from early November to mid-March, and Pacific Daylight Time (PDT, UTC-7) from mid-March to early November. Eastern Time runs 3 hours ahead. Central Time runs 2 hours ahead. Mountain Time runs 1 hour ahead. The best window for calls is 9 AM to 5 PM PT.

## Is a 424 Area Code Call a Scam?

The **424 area code** is real and legitimate. But its name recognition makes it a common target for **caller ID spoofing**. Scammers fake a local area code so more people pick up.

### Common 424-Spoofed Scams

**Student loan forgiveness robocalls** ask for personal details, claiming they need to change your loan status. **Fake bank fraud alerts** pretend to be major LA-area banks. They ask you to move funds to a "safe account." **Utility payment fraud** pretends to be LADWP or SoCalGas. It demands quick payment with a prepaid card.

### Protecting Yourself and Your Business

Real agencies and utilities never demand instant payment by gift card or cryptocurrency on a surprise call. Hang up. Then call the organization back using the official number on their website. Businesses should use a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider. This verifies caller ID data and cuts down on spoofing.
