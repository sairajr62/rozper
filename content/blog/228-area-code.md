## History of the 228 Area Code

The North American Numbering Plan launched in 1947. At that time, Mississippi got one single code — **601** — for the whole state. Demand grew fast through the 1990s. Fax machines, pagers, and mobile phones all used up 601's supply.

### The 1997 Split: 228 Created

Area code 228 split off from 601 on **September 15, 1997**. This carved out the three southernmost Gulf Coast counties. In 1999, a second split created **662** for northern Mississippi.

### No Overlay — 228 Remains Sole Code

The **228 area code** has never needed an overlay. So every Gulf Coast number still carries the same 228 prefix. For regional business info and state resources, visit the [State of Mississippi](https://www.ms.gov/) official portal.

## 228 Area Code Time Zone

![Optimal Calling Hours in Central Time Zone for Gulf Coast Businesses](/images/blog/228-image-3.webp)

The 228 area code zone runs on **Central Time (CT)**. That means CST (UTC−6) from early November to mid-March. It switches to CDT (UTC−5) from mid-March to early November.

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**East Coast (ET):** Eastern Time runs 1 hour ahead of Central. A 9 AM CT call reaches New York at 10 AM ET.

**West Coast (PT):** Pacific Time runs 2 hours behind Central. Wait until after 11 AM CT before you call West Coast clients.

**International:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 228 + 7-digit number; on mobile: +1-228-XXX-XXXX.

## Is a 228 Area Code Call a Scam?

The area code 228 prefix is real. It serves real residents and businesses. But like all US codes, scammers can spoof it. They fake a local caller ID to get more people to answer.

### Health Insurance Robocalls

The most common scam uses taped calls. They offer cheap "government" health plans. They aim at people in Mississippi with no coverage, since more people here lack coverage than in most other states. Real insurers do not just call you out of the blue.

### Auto Warranty Fraud

Robocalls often claim your car warranty is about to expire. They demand payment right away. Many of these come from spoofed 228 numbers. Real warranty companies contact you in writing.

### Medicare and Medicaid Impersonation

Scammers pose as CMS agents. They ask for payment or account details. They aim at Mississippi's older coastal residents, many of whom use Medicaid. Real agencies never call out of nowhere to ask for your personal data.

### Staying Protected

Hang up on calls you did not expect. Then dial the agency's published number yourself. Businesses should use a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider. This verifies outbound caller ID. Report suspicious calls at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
