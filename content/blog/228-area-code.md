## History of the 228 Area Code

When the North American Numbering Plan launched in 1947, Mississippi was assigned a single code — **601** — for the entire state. Growing demand from fax machines, pagers, and mobile phones through the 1990s depleted 601's supply.

### The 1997 Split: 228 Created

Area code 228 was split from 601 on **September 15, 1997**, carving out the three southernmost Gulf Coast counties. In 1999, a second split created **662** for northern Mississippi.

### No Overlay — 228 Remains Sole Code

The **228 area code** has never required an overlay, so every Gulf Coast number carries the same 228 prefix. For regional business information and state resources, visit the [State of Mississippi](https://www.ms.gov/) official portal.

The 228 area code zone operates in the **Central Time Zone (CT)**: CST (UTC−6) from early November to mid-March, and CDT (UTC−5) from mid-March to early November.

### Cross-Timezone Calling Guide

**East Coast (ET):** Eastern Time runs 1 hour ahead of Central — a 9 AM CT call arrives in New York at 10 AM ET.

**West Coast (PT):** Pacific Time is 2 hours behind Central. Wait until after 11 AM CT before contacting West Coast clients.

**International:** From the UK, dial 00 + 1 + 228 + 7-digit number; on mobile: +1-228-XXX-XXXX.

## Is a 228 Area Code Call a Scam?

The area code 228 prefix is legitimate, serving real residents and businesses. Like all US codes, it is subject to caller ID spoofing by scammers who display local numbers to improve answer rates.

### Health Insurance Robocalls

The most common scam involves prerecorded calls offering subsidized "government" health insurance targeting uninsured residents present in Mississippi at above-national-average rates. Legitimate insurers do not cold-call without a prior inquiry.

### Auto Warranty Fraud

Robocalls claiming a vehicle warranty is expiring and demanding immediate payment are widely reported on spoofed 228 numbers. Genuine warranty departments contact customers in writing.

### Medicare and Medicaid Impersonation

Scammers impersonate CMS agents requesting payment or account verification, exploiting Mississippi's older coastal population and high Medicaid enrollment. Real agencies do not initiate unsolicited calls requesting personal information.

### Staying Protected

Hang up on unsolicited calls and dial the agency's published number directly. Businesses should use a **STIR/SHAKEN-compliant** VoIP provider to authenticate outbound caller ID and report suspicious calls at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
