## What Is the 909 Area Code?

![Map highlighting San Bernardino County and the Inland Empire served by the 909 prefix](/images/blog/what-is-the-909-area-code.webp)

The **909 area code** is a North American Numbering Plan (NANP) code. It serves the Inland Empire in Southern California. It covers most of San Bernardino County. It reaches into eastern Los Angeles County too. It also touches small parts of Riverside County near the border.

The code was created in November 1992. It split off from the older 714 code, which had covered much of Southern California since the 1950s. The Inland Empire was growing fast. The 714 numbering pool was running low. A new code became the only fix.

Today 909 shares its territory with the 840 overlay code. The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) added 840 in February 2021. It handles new phone numbers. Every existing 909 number stayed exactly the same.

## How the 909 Code Evolved: A Brief History

![Timeline illustration of the 1992 split from 714 and the 2021 addition of the 840 overlay](/images/blog/how-the-909-code-evolved.webp)

The story of 909 starts in the early 1990s. California was growing fast, and phone numbers were running out.

**1992: Birth of a New Code**
On November 14, 1992, the **909 area code** went live. It split off from the 714 code. The Inland Empire had used 714 since the 1950s. Growth in the region made a new code necessary. The split gave the area its own phone identity.

**2004: The 951 Branch**
Western Riverside County kept growing on its own path. In 2004 it split away from 909 and got its own code, [951 area code](/blog/951-area-code/). This narrowed the borders of **area code 909**. It also freed up room for more numbers. San Bernardino County stayed at the core of 909.

**2021: The 840 Overlay**
By the late 2010s, 909 was running low on numbers again. The CPUC approved an overlay. The new 840 code went live on February 23, 2021. New customers began getting 840 numbers. Every existing 909 number stayed the same.

## Time Zone and Dialing Instructions for Area Code 909

Every number in this region runs on **Pacific Time (PT)**. That's UTC−8 in standard time and UTC−7 during daylight saving. This matches Los Angeles, San Diego, and San Francisco. Scheduling calls across California is simple.

**10-Digit Dialing Is Now Mandatory**
The 840 overlay changed how people dial. Since February 2021, every call in the 909 region needs 10 digits. Even local calls need the full area code plus the 7-digit number.

**Calling In and Out**
From the US, dial 1-909-XXX-XXXX. From abroad, add your country's exit code first, then 1-909-XXX-XXXX. Anyone who gets a call from a **909 area code** number will see "909" on their caller ID.

## Recognizing and Avoiding 909 Scam Calls

Not every call from a **909 area code** number is real. Scammers spoof local codes to look trustworthy. The 909 region has a large, diverse population, which makes it an easy target.

Common scam types reported in the 909 region include:

- **Home improvement solicitations** – Unwanted calls about solar panels, roofing, or HVAC
- **Auto warranty robocalls** – False claims that your car warranty is about to expire
- **Fake IRS or legal threats** – Callers posing as officials and threatening arrest
- **Tech support fraud** – Callers claiming your device or account has been hacked

According to the [Wikipedia article on area codes 909 and 840](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_codes_909_and_840), the region's large size and dense population make it a steady target for caller ID spoofing.

To stay safe: let unknown calls go to voicemail. Use a reverse phone lookup before calling back. Report scam calls to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
