# 209 Area Code: Central California Cities & Business Guide

California's Central Valley is one of the most productive farming and business regions in the United States. This area code is the phone prefix that connects it. The 209 region covers five counties. It has more than 1.5 million residents. It spans Stockton, Modesto, Tracy, Merced, and many nearby towns. Maybe you are tracing an unfamiliar call. Maybe you are expanding into the Central Valley. ## What Is the 209 Area Code?

![What Is the 209 Area Code?](/images/blog/what-is-the-209-area-code.webp)

The **209 area code** is the phone prefix for California's Central Valley. It is part of the North American Numbering Plan (NANP). It was set up in 1958 after a split from the original 415 area code. It covers five main counties: San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Merced, Calaveras, and Tuolumne.

This region covers a lot of ground. It runs from the rich farm valleys of San Joaquin and Stanislaus counties to mountain towns in the Sierra Nevada foothills. Major cities include Stockton, Modesto, Tracy, Merced, Turlock, Manteca, Lodi, and Ceres. Each one plays its own role in the Central Valley's varied economy.

In 2022, the **350 area code** was added as an overlay for the same area. Both codes serve the same counties. The only difference is the three-digit prefix. Just to the north, the [916 area code](/blog/916-area-code/) serves Sacramento. Many businesses target both regions together as one Northern California strategy.

## History of the 209 Area Code

![History of the 209 Area Code](/images/blog/history-of-the-209-area-code.webp)

The **209 area code** was created in 1958. It was carved out of the original 415 prefix, which used to serve all of Northern California. Back then, the 209 territory stretched from Stockton in the north down to Fresno in the south. That was a huge inland corridor with millions of residents.

Central California's population grew fast through the 1980s and 1990s. Demand for new phone numbers grew too, and it outpaced the original supply. In 1997, regulators split the region. The southern part — including Fresno, Visalia, and the valley below Merced — moved to the newly created **559 area code**. This set the current 209 boundaries: San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Merced, Calaveras, and Tuolumne counties.

The cities served by the **area code 209** today show the full range of the Central Valley economy:

- **Stockton** — the region's largest city and a busy inland port on the San Joaquin River delta.
- **Modesto** — a hub for food plants, wine, and retail.
- **Tracy** — one of California's fastest-growing shipping hubs near the Bay Area.
- **Merced** — home to UC Merced, the newest campus in the UC system.
- **Turlock** — a major trade and farming center in Stanislaus County.
- **Manteca** — a fast-growing city along the Highway 99 corridor.
- **Lodi** — a well-known wine region in northern San Joaquin County.
- **Sonora** — the county seat of Tuolumne County, in the Sierra Nevada foothills.
- **Angels Camp** — an old Gold Rush town in Calaveras County.

The region has grown into a mixed business economy. It now spans logistics, healthcare, and technology. That growth keeps demand for local phone numbers steady across every business sector.

## 209 Area Code Time Zone and the 350 Overlay

![209 Area Code Time Zone and the 350 Overlay](/images/blog/209-area-code-time-zone-and-the-350-overlay.webp)

All phone numbers in the **209 area code** region run on **Pacific Time (PT)**. In winter, the zone follows Pacific Standard Time (PST, UTC−8). From spring through fall — usually mid-March to early November — the region follows Pacific Daylight Time (PDT, UTC−7).

Here is how that lines up for businesses booking calls from other time zones:

- A 9:00 a.m. Stockton meeting is **12:00 p.m. Eastern Time (ET)**.
- That same 9:00 a.m. call lands at **5:00 p.m. in London (GMT/BST)**.
- The Central Valley runs **3 hours behind** the US East Coast. It runs **8 hours behind** most of Western Europe.

**The 350 Overlay Explained**

In 2022, the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) added the **350 area code**. It works as a number-relief overlay, since the 209 pool had run low. Regulators could have split the region again, but that would have forced many businesses and residents to change their numbers. Instead, they chose the overlay approach. All existing 209 subscribers kept their numbers. New numbers started drawing from the 350 pool.

Since the overlay launched, **mandatory 10-digit dialing** applies to every call in the territory. This is true whether the number carries 209 or 350. Seven-digit dialing no longer connects. Update all customer-facing materials to the full 10-digit format. This includes voicemail greetings, websites, and business cards. For the official federal rules behind this requirement, see the FCC's [ten-digit dialing guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/ten-digit-dialing).
