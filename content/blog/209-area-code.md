# 209 Area Code: Central California Cities & Business Guide

California's Central Valley is one of the most productive agricultural and commercial regions in the United States, and the **209 area code** is the telephone prefix that connects it. Covering five counties and more than 1.5 million residents, the 209 region spans Stockton, Modesto, Tracy, Merced, and dozens of surrounding communities. Whether you are tracing an unfamiliar call, expanding into the Central Valley, or looking for a virtual local number, this guide covers everything — geography, history, time zone, overlay rules, and how to get your own 209 number.

## What Is the 209 Area Code?

![What Is the 209 Area Code?](/images/blog/what-is-the-209-area-code.webp)

The **209 area code** is the telephone prefix assigned to California's Central Valley under the North American Numbering Plan (NANP). Established in 1958 following a split from the original 415 area code, it covers five primary counties: San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Merced, Calaveras, and Tuolumne.

This region spans a broad geographic range — from the fertile agricultural valleys of San Joaquin and Stanislaus counties to the mountain communities nestled in the Sierra Nevada foothills. Major population centers include Stockton, Modesto, Tracy, Merced, Turlock, Manteca, Lodi, and Ceres, each of which plays a distinct role in the Central Valley's diverse economy.

In 2022, the **350 area code** was introduced as an overlay covering the same footprint. Both codes serve identical counties — the only distinction is the three-digit prefix. Just to the north, the [916 area code](/blog/916-area-code/) serves Sacramento, and many businesses target both regions as a complementary Northern California strategy.

## History of the 209 Area Code

![History of the 209 Area Code](/images/blog/history-of-the-209-area-code.webp)

The **209 area code** was created in 1958, carved out of the original 415 prefix that previously served all of Northern California. At the time, the 209 territory stretched from Stockton in the north down to Fresno in the south — a vast inland corridor covering millions of residents.

As Central California's population grew through the 1980s and 1990s, demand for new phone numbers outpaced the original footprint. In 1997, regulators split the region: the southern portion — including Fresno, Visalia, and the valley below Merced — was reassigned to the newly created **559 area code**, establishing the current 209 boundaries across San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Merced, Calaveras, and Tuolumne counties.

The cities served by the **area code 209** today reflect the full breadth of the Central Valley economy:

- **Stockton** — the region's largest city and a major inland port on the San Joaquin River delta
- **Modesto** — hub of food processing, wine production, and regional retail
- **Tracy** — one of California's fastest-growing logistics and distribution centers near the Bay Area
- **Merced** — home to UC Merced, California's newest University of California campus
- **Turlock** — a major commercial and agricultural center in Stanislaus County
- **Manteca** — a rapidly expanding city along the Highway 99 corridor
- **Lodi** — an internationally recognized wine appellation in northern San Joaquin County
- **Sonora** — county seat of Tuolumne County in the Sierra Nevada foothills
- **Angels Camp** — a historic Gold Rush–era town in Calaveras County

The region's evolution into a diversified commercial economy spanning logistics, healthcare, and technology has sustained consistent demand for **209 area code** numbers across every business sector.

## 209 Area Code Time Zone and the 350 Overlay

![209 Area Code Time Zone and the 350 Overlay](/images/blog/209-area-code-time-zone-and-the-350-overlay.webp)

All phone numbers within the **209 area code** region operate on **Pacific Time (PT)**. During winter months, the zone follows Pacific Standard Time (PST, UTC−8). From spring through autumn — typically mid-March to early November — the region observes Pacific Daylight Time (PDT, UTC−7).

For businesses scheduling calls with Central Valley contacts from other time zones:

- A 9:00 a.m. Stockton meeting is **12:00 p.m. Eastern Time (ET)**
- The same 9:00 a.m. call falls at **5:00 p.m. in London (GMT/BST)**
- The Central Valley runs **3 hours behind** the US East Coast and **8 hours behind** most of Western Europe

**The 350 Overlay Explained**

In 2022, the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) introduced the **350 area code** as a number-relief overlay for the exhausted 209 pool. Rather than executing another geographic split — which would have forced many businesses and residents to change their numbers — regulators chose the overlay approach: all existing 209 subscribers kept their numbers, while new numbers began drawing from the 350 pool.

Since the overlay launched, **mandatory 10-digit dialing** applies to every call within the territory, whether the number carries 209 or 350. Seven-digit dialing no longer connects. Update all customer-facing materials — voicemail greetings, websites, and business cards — to the full 10-digit format. For the official federal rules behind this requirement, see the FCC's [ten-digit dialing guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/ten-digit-dialing).
