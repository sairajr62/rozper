# 380 Area Code: Complete Guide to Columbus, Ohio's Local Number

Every phone number tells a story about where a business operates. ## What Is the 380 Area Code?

The **380 area code** is an overlay for the original 614 area code, both of which serve central Ohio. When 614 began running low on available number combinations, telecommunications regulators assigned 380 as a relief code — meaning both codes now cover the same geographic region simultaneously.

Callers see no functional difference between a 614 and a 380 number; both connect to the same cities and neighborhoods. The primary effect for residents and businesses was a shift to mandatory 10-digit dialing for all local calls — even calling a neighbor next door now requires the full area code before the seven digits.

Unlike a geographic split — where two codes divide a region into separate halves — an overlay preserves continuity and allows existing 614 subscribers to keep their numbers unchanged without any service disruption.

## How the 380 Area Code Came to Be

Central Ohio's rapid population and business growth in the early 2000s placed mounting pressure on the 614 numbering inventory. Rising demand from new mobile subscribers, business phone lines, and internet-based VoIP services accelerated the depletion of available number combinations faster than regulators initially projected.

In 2001, the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) approved **area code 380** as an overlay solution to address the shortage. The code was officially activated on February 27, 2016, adding a fresh pool of number combinations for the region without displacing existing customers.

Residents and businesses were not reassigned new numbers — instead, the **380 area code** became available for all new activations alongside the existing 614 numbers. The 10-digit dialing requirement that accompanied the change applies uniformly across both codes.

## 380 Area Code Time Zone and Dialing Rules

Numbers in this region follow **Eastern Standard Time (EST)**, or UTC-5, shifting to Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC-4) during summer months. This is a practical detail for businesses coordinating calls across time zones — a 9:00 a.m. meeting in Columbus is 6:00 a.m. on the West Coast and 2:00 p.m. in London.

Since 2016, the **380 area code** and its 614 counterpart both require mandatory 10-digit dialing for all calls placed within the service area. Dialing only seven digits will not connect — every local call requires the full prefix.

Knowing the time zone and dialing rules for **area code 380** helps prevent scheduling errors and missed connections, whether you are coordinating from within Ohio or managing a remote team from across the country.
