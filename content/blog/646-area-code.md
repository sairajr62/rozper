## What Is the 646 Area Code?

![What Is the 646 Area Code](/images/blog/What Is the 646 Area Code.webp)

The **646 area code** is a telephone area code in the North American Numbering Plan (NANP). It is assigned only to the borough of Manhattan, New York City. It works as an overlay code, sharing Manhattan's ground with the older 212 code instead of splitting the area in two. It launched in 1999. Demand from landlines, mobile phones, and business lines had outgrown the 212 supply. **Area code 646** grew the pool of Manhattan numbers to meet that demand.

Today, Manhattan runs four active codes at once: 212, 646, 332, and 917. All four are real Manhattan numbers. All four need the full 10-digit dial. The **646 area code** is one of the most recognized New York City codes, right alongside its overlay partners.

## A Brief Timeline of Manhattan Area Codes

![A Brief Timeline of Manhattan Area Codes](/images/blog/A Brief Timeline of Manhattan Area Codes.webp)

The **646 area code** was born out of need. Manhattan's phone network faced huge demand through the 1990s. Fax machines, early cell networks, corporate lines, and new internet-era businesses pushed demand far past what the 212 code alone could handle.

Key Manhattan area code milestones:

- **1947:** Area code 212 assigned to all of New York State under the NANP.
- **1984:** Outer boroughs split off to 718; 212 became Manhattan-only.
- **1992:** Area code 917 introduced as the first mobile and pager overlay for all five boroughs.
- **1999:** The **646 area code** launched as a general overlay. It required mandatory 10-digit dialing across Manhattan right away.
- **2017:** Area code 332 activated as a fourth relief overlay for new Manhattan assignments.

The launch of **area code 646** marked a turning point in how New York City managed its number supply. Our [347 area code](/blog/347-area-code) guide covers the same story for Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. Their overlay also began in 1999.

## Time Zone, Format, and 10-Digit Dialing Requirements

All **646 area code** numbers operate in the **Eastern Time Zone (ET)**:

- **Eastern Standard Time (EST):** UTC−5, from early November through mid-March
- **Eastern Daylight Time (EDT):** UTC−4, from mid-March through early November

If your business is in Pacific Time and you call Manhattan during normal business hours (9 AM–6 PM ET), your outreach window is 6 AM to 3 PM PT.

A full 10-digit dial is a fixed rule for the **646 area code**, because of Manhattan's overlay setup. Four codes share the same ground, so every call, local or long-distance, must use the full format: 1 + 646 + 7-digit local number. Dial only 7 digits and the call will fail. The [FCC's consumer ten-digit dialing guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/ten-digit-dialing) is the official US government reference for these rules.

- **Register your number** on the FCC's Do Not Call list to cut down on unwanted sales calls.
