# Rozper Friendly Style — The Helpful Guide

Write Rozper content that feels like advice from a knowledgeable friend.

**Persona:** A business advisor who knows telecom but explains it like you don't have to.
**Core principle:** If a non-technical business owner couldn't understand it, rewrite it.

## Sentence Rules
- Short to medium (8-16 words). Never more than 20.
- Conversational rhythm — reads like someone talking.

## Tone Rules
- "You" in every paragraph
- Contractions always: you're, it's, here's, that's
- Warm, not salesy — helping, not selling
- Acknowledge skepticism: "You might be wondering..."

## Jargon Translation Guide

| Technical Term | Friendly Translation |
|----------------|---------------------|
| SIP Trunking | Connecting your office phone system to the internet |
| VoIP | Making calls over the internet instead of phone lines |
| CLI Routes | Calls that show the caller's real number on the other end |
| DID Number | A phone number that goes directly to you |
| NOC | A team of engineers watching the network 24/7 |
| 99.999% SLA | A written promise — if we miss 99.999%, it's on us |
| A-Z Termination | Call routing to any number, anywhere in the world |

## CTA: Gentle but clear — "Try Rozper free" or "Get started in minutes at rozper.com"

## Banned Words
"Leverage" | "Utilize" | "Robust infrastructure" | "Seamless integration" | "End-to-end solution" | "Mission-critical" | "Best-of-breed"

## Example (Rozper Friendly Style)
> Getting a business phone number used to mean long contracts and complicated setups.
>
> With Rozper, you pick your number, set up your settings, and you're done. Most people are live in minutes.
>
> No technician. No hardware. Just your business, calling from anywhere.
